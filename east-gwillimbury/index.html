Builder-grade HVAC problems and rural heating costs we fix

These are the issues behind most calls we get from East Gwillimbury.

  • The house is three years old and the upstairs already overheats

    Builders often install the smallest system that passes inspection, with too few returns on the second floor. We check airflow room by room, add returns where needed and, if the equipment is undersized, quote a proper upgrade.

  • The builder furnace is noisy and runs in short bursts

    Single-stage builder furnaces start at full blast every time. A two-stage, variable-speed furnace like the Lennox ML296V runs on low most of the time, so it's quieter and the heat is steadier.

  • The propane truck shows up more often than you'd like

    A cold-climate heat pump moves heat from outdoor air even well below freezing, so it can carry most of your heating. The propane furnace stays as backup for the deepest cold.

  • Rooms on the windward side of the farmhouse never warm up

    Wind off the fields pulls heat from exposed walls fast. We size from a heat-loss calculation that accounts for that exposure and add a ductless head where the ducts can't keep up.

  • The builder AC gives up in a July heat wave

    Entry-level ACs are often sized tight. We compare the heat gain with the unit's capacity and, when it's time, replace it with a heat pump that cools in summer and heats in winter.

  • An older furnace in Mount Albert is living on borrowed time

    Our TSSA-certified gas techs check the heat exchanger, venting and repair history, then give you a straight repair or replace answer with a written price.

  • The builder's AC sits on gravel that has already started to sink

    New subdivision lots settle for years after the house goes up, and a tilted condenser wears its compressor and fan unevenly. When we replace the unit, it goes on a proper levelled pad or a wall bracket, raised clear of the drifting snow that comes off open fields around Queensville and Sharon.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Before you replace builder equipment in a new East Gwillimbury home

A furnace that's only a few years old might still be covered by its manufacturer, and a few checks can save you from replacing something that just needs setting up properly.

  • Find the model and serial number on the furnace and check whether the warranty was ever registered. Builders often skip this step, and the clock may still be running.
  • Ask us to measure static pressure and airflow at each register. Uneven rooms in a new build are more often a duct and damper problem than an equipment problem.
  • Check the blower speed settings. Many builder furnaces are left on a default that's wrong for the house.
  • Look at the filter slot. A one-inch filter that clogs in weeks is the cheapest upgrade in the house.

If the equipment really is undersized or failing, we say so and quote the upgrade. If it isn't, you pay for a tune-up and some adjustments, and the new furnace waits until it's actually needed.

Frequently asked questions

Does a builder-grade furnace in East Gwillimbury need replacing right away?

Not always. Many builder systems just need airflow fixes, added returns or balancing, and we'll tell you whether the equipment itself is undersized before suggesting a replacement.

Can a heat pump replace most of my propane heating?

Yes, in most cases. A cold-climate heat pump rated to -30°C can carry most of the heating season, and the propane furnace stays as backup for the deepest cold.

What rebate can a propane-heated home get?

Homes heated by propane, oil, electricity or wood can qualify for up to $7,500 through the Home Renovation Savings Program, based on heat pump size. The pre-approval needs to come through before we install anything.

Is it normal for a new Queensville home to have uneven temperatures?

It's common, but it's not something you have to live with. Builder systems often have too few returns upstairs or unbalanced ducts, and both can be fixed.

Who looks after furnace repair in East Gwillimbury's newer subdivisions?

We do. Our TSSA-certified gas technicians repair builder furnaces in Queensville and Sharon as well as propane units on rural lots, and you get a written price before the work starts.

Which areas do your HVAC services in East Gwillimbury cover?

All of it, including Queensville, Sharon, Holland Landing, Mount Albert and the rural roads in between.

How much does a heat pump cost for a propane-heated home in East Gwillimbury?

A cold-climate heat pump in East Gwillimbury starts from $5,500 installed, before a rebate that can reach $7,500 for propane-heated homes. A high-efficiency furnace starts from $3,300. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, and the quote is guaranteed in writing.
