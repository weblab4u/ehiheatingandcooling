Snowbelt facts that shape a Sault Ste. Marie system

The Sault gets heavy lake-effect snow and a long heating season. Those two facts drive almost every decision we make here.

  • SnowfallLake Superior snowbelt

    Lake-effect storms can bury an outdoor unit or a furnace vent overnight, so placement and venting get planned before anything else.

  • UtilitiesPUC Distribution and Enbridge Gas

    We compare your PUC hydro rate with your gas or fuel costs before deciding how hard the heat pump should work.

  • HomesCore, post-war and edge-of-town

    Older homes Downtown and in the West End, post-war bungalows, and homes toward Goulais River and Prince Township, some off the gas line.

  • RebatesUp to $7,500

Sault Ste. Marie homes we work on

Most homes in and around the Sault fit one of these four groups, and we plan each one differently.

Gas-heated homes in town

A dependable furnace is the priority. Add a heat pump in place of the AC and it carries much of the winter, with the furnace covering the hardest cold.

Homes past the gas line

Out toward Goulais River and Prince Township, homes on propane, oil or baseboards can switch to a cold-climate heat pump with electric or fuel backup.

Compact post-war houses

Compact layouts, older ducts and modest attics. We check insulation and duct sizing so the new system matches what the house really loses.

Lots that collect heavy snow

Steep roofs, narrow side yards and driveways that need a lot of clearing. Unit placement gets planned around where the snow ends up.

Snow and cold problems our Sault Ste. Marie HVAC technicians fix

These are the calls that come in after the big storms. Each one has a cause, and a fix that keeps it from coming back.

  • Snow sliding off the roof lands right on the outdoor unit

    A roof avalanche can bend the fan guard and crush the coil fins. We move the unit out from under the eave line, onto a tall stand or wall bracket, where falling snow misses it.

  • After a heavy snowfall, the furnace locks out

    High-efficiency furnaces vent through plastic pipes low on the side of the house, and a night of lake-effect snow can cover them. We check the termination height and, where code allows, extend the pipes upward with proper supports and clearances.

  • You dig out a path to the heat pump every morning

    That means it's sitting too low or in the wrong spot. We raise it above your usual snow depth, keep it out of the path of the snowblower's throw, and choose the side of the house that drifts least.

  • The heat pump goes into defrost often and the air feels cool

    Frequent defrost is normal in wet, snowy weather. With backup heat sized correctly, the house holds its temperature through each cycle. We also check sensors, settings and the drain path so meltwater gets away.

  • A furnace that has seen 20 winters is starting to slip

    Our TSSA-certified gas techs inspect the heat exchanger, ignitor and blower, then give you a repair price and a replacement price. If it's time, a two-stage, variable-speed furnace from 96% AFUE is quieter and burns less.

  • Electric baseboards in a Korah home cost too much to run

    A ductless or ducted cold-climate heat pump delivers the same comfort on much less electricity, and baseboard-heated homes can qualify for the higher rebate tier.

  • A storm knocks the power out and you want heat that keeps going

    A furnace and a heat pump both need electricity, but a gas furnace draws very little of it. We put the furnace on its own circuit so a portable generator and a properly installed transfer switch, handled through ESA-licensed electrical work, can keep it running during an outage. Tell us at the quote stage and we plan the wiring for it.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Frequently asked questions

Where should a heat pump go on a house in Sault Ste. Marie?

On a raised stand or wall bracket, away from roof edges and away from where you pile snow off the driveway. We pick the spot on site after looking at your roof lines and how snow drifts on your lot.

Why does my furnace stop working after a snowstorm?

Usually because snow has covered the furnace's intake or exhaust pipe, which trips a safety switch. Clear the pipes gently, and if it keeps happening we can look at raising them where code allows.

Can a heat pump handle a Sault Ste. Marie winter?

A cold-climate heat pump rated to -30°C can carry most of a Sault winter when it's sized from a heat-loss calculation. We always pair it with backup heat for the coldest stretches.

How much is the heat pump rebate in Sault Ste. Marie?

Homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood can qualify for up to $7,500, and Enbridge gas-heated homes commonly qualify for up to $2,000. Get the pre-approval sorted before the install, since the program can change its terms.

Do you work in Goulais River and Prince Township?

Yes. Our crews serve homes in Goulais River, Prince Township and across the Sault area, and we set dates with you directly.

Is furnace repair in Sault Ste. Marie something you handle?

Yes. Our TSSA-certified gas technicians repair and service furnaces in Sault Ste. Marie, and we take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays at 1-647-694-5845.

What will a new furnace cost in Sault Ste. Marie?

A high-efficiency gas furnace starts from $3,300 installed. Vent extensions for deep snow, a condensate pump or a larger blower add to that, and all of it is listed on a guaranteed written quote before we start.
