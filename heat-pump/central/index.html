Problems a central heat pump solves

Ducted heat pump calls usually start here.

  • Two aging systems, two repair bills coming

    A 15-year-old furnace and a 12-year-old AC are a matched pair in one way only: they're both about to cost you. A ducted heat pump replaces both with one outdoor unit and one indoor unit on the ducts you have.

  • You want a heat pump but don't want to tear up walls

    No need. A central heat pump uses your supply and return ducts exactly as the furnace did. The work happens in the furnace room and outside, not in your living room.

  • Hot upstairs, cold basement

    Often a duct problem the furnace hid by blasting. We measure static pressure and airflow, fix the undersized return or leaky trunk, and the variable-speed heat pump runs long and low to even things out.

  • Propane, oil or an electric furnace is draining the budget

    A central heat pump with an electric air handler cuts heating cost sharply against resistance heat or delivered fuel, and these homes get the top rebate tier.

  • On Enbridge gas and worried about -25°C nights

    Keep a furnace in the mix. A hybrid system puts the heat pump coil on a new or existing gas furnace and switches over automatically below the balance point.

  • You've been quoted a heat pump without anyone looking at the ducts

    That's the quote to walk away from. Ductwork that's fine for a furnace can choke a heat pump, which moves more air at lower temperatures. We check first. See our heat pump installation process.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Will my ductwork work with a central heat pump?

Usually yes, but we check rather than assume. A heat pump delivers air at roughly 35°C to 45°C instead of the 50°C to 60°C a gas furnace pushes out, so it moves more air to deliver the same heat. Ducts sized for a furnace sometimes can't move that air without high static pressure, which means noise, lost capacity and a shorter equipment life.

On the assessment visit we measure total external static pressure with a manometer and compare it to the equipment's rated limit (typically 0.5 inches of water column for residential air handlers). We look for undersized returns, crushed flex runs and leaky trunk joints. Most Ontario homes need nothing more than a larger return drop or a few sealed joints. Where ducts really are too small, we tell you the fix and the cost up front rather than let the system struggle.

Three ways to build a ducted heat pump system

Same outdoor unit, three indoor choices. Which one fits depends on your fuel, your panel and what's already in the furnace room.

Heat pump + matched electric air handler

Midea EVOX G3 outdoor unit with a matched MAAHE or MAUHE air handler and electric auxiliary strips. All-electric, and the highest rebate tier for non-gas homes.

Learn more

Heat pump + gas furnace coil (hybrid)

The heat pump's indoor coil sits on a new Lennox ML296V, EL297V or Midea MGH96 furnace, or on a sound existing furnace. The furnace blower moves the air and covers deep cold.

Learn more

Replace the AC, keep the furnace

If your furnace is under about ten years old with a variable-speed blower, we swap the AC for a heat pump condenser and coil. The lowest entry cost into heat pump heating.

What's included in a central heat pump installation

  • Manual J style heat-loss calculation and duct static pressure test before sizing
  • Removal and disposal of the old furnace, AC or air handler
  • Matched indoor unit (air handler or furnace coil) set on the existing plenum, new transitions sealed with mastic
  • New refrigerant line set, pressure tested with nitrogen and pulled to a deep vacuum before charging
  • Outdoor unit on a riser stand above the snow line, with clearances for airflow and defrost drainage
  • Dedicated 240V circuit, disconnect and panel check by ESA-licensed electricians
  • Condensate drain with a trap, and a secondary pan where the unit sits above finished space
  • Heat-pump-ready thermostat, commissioning in heating, cooling and defrost, and a walk-through
  • Warranty registration, permits where required, and rebate paperwork filed

Frequently asked questions

What is a central heat pump?

A central heat pump, also called a ducted heat pump, is a single outdoor unit connected to an indoor air handler or furnace coil that heats and cools the whole house through the home's ductwork. It replaces both a furnace and a central air conditioner with one system.

Can a central heat pump use my existing ductwork?

In most Ontario homes, yes. We measure static pressure and airflow first, because a heat pump moves more air at a lower temperature than a furnace. Most homes need at most a larger return or some sealing; a few need duct changes, and we quote those up front.

How much does central heat pump installation cost in Ontario?

Central heat pump installation with EHI starts at $5,500 installed for a 2 to 2.5 ton system on existing ducts. Larger sizes, a new air handler or furnace, and electrical upgrades move the price up, and the Ontario rebate of up to $7,500 comes back after. Your quote is guaranteed before work starts.

What size central heat pump do I need?

Most Ontario homes up to about 2,000 square feet fall in the 2 to 3 ton range, and larger two-storey homes often need 4 to 5 ton. The right size comes from a heat-loss calculation that accounts for insulation, windows and air leakage. Oversizing causes short cycling and poor dehumidification.

Can I keep my gas furnace with a central heat pump?

Yes. A hybrid (dual fuel) set-up puts the heat pump coil on your furnace and uses the furnace blower. The thermostat runs the heat pump most of the time and switches to gas below a set outdoor temperature. It is the lowest-running-cost option for Enbridge gas homes.

Is a ducted heat pump noisy indoors?

No louder than a modern furnace, and often quieter, because variable-speed blowers run at low speed most of the time. Outdoors, inverter-driven units like the Midea EVOX G3 and Kepler are quieter than most older central air conditioners.
