What is a cold climate heat pump?

A cold climate heat pump is an air-source heat pump designed and tested to deliver most of its rated heating capacity at -15°C and to keep operating at -25°C to -30°C, where a standard heat pump would have lost half its output or shut down. In Ontario that's the difference between a system that carries January and one that hands the job to backup heat every night.

Two pieces of engineering make it work. An inverter-driven compressor speeds up as it gets colder outside, pulling more heat from the air instead of falling behind like a fixed-speed unit. Vapour injection feeds a small amount of partly condensed refrigerant back into the compressor mid-cycle, which boosts capacity in deep cold without overheating the compressor. The result is real heat at -25°C in Barrie or Sudbury, not just warm-ish air.

Problems a cold climate heat pump solves

  • Your current heat pump gives up below -5°C

    Older and budget heat pumps lose capacity fast once it's cold, so the backup strips or furnace run all winter and the savings never show up. A cold climate model keeps its output where Ontario winters actually live, from -10°C to -25°C.

  • Propane or oil deliveries are eating your budget

    Homes outside the Enbridge gas grid, from Wasaga Beach to Pembroke, pay the most to heat. A cold climate heat pump delivers two to three units of heat per unit of electricity and gets the top rebate tier, up to $7,500.

  • You've heard heat pumps don't work in Northern Ontario

    They didn't, fifteen years ago. The EVOX G3 and Kepler are rated to -30°C. For North Bay, Timmins and anywhere the mercury sits below -30°C for days, we pair the heat pump with a gas furnace or an electric air handler with auxiliary heat.

  • Furnace and AC are both near the end

    One cold climate heat pump replaces both, with one warranty and one outdoor unit, and it uses your existing ducts in a central installation.

  • You want the rebate but the rules are confusing

    The rebate hinges on one thing: the model must be on NRCan's qualified products list. We only install qualified equipment, confirm eligibility with you before installation, and file the paperwork. See the rebate details.

HSPF2, SEER2 and COP in plain words

Spec sheets throw numbers at you. Here's what each one tells you about your hydro bill.

RatingWhat it measuresWhat it means for you
HSPF2Heating efficiency across a whole heating season, under the current test methodHigher is better. It's the number that predicts your winter hydro bill.
SEER2Cooling efficiency across a summerHigher is better. It matters for July, but in Ontario HSPF2 matters more.
COPHeat delivered per unit of electricity at one outdoor temperatureA COP of 2.5 at -15°C means 2.5 units of heat for every unit of hydro. Baseboards are always 1.0.
Capacity at -15°CHow many BTU the unit still produces when it's coldThe number that matters for sizing. Look for most of the rated capacity, not a steep drop.

Why the NRCan qualified list matters

The Home Renovation Savings Program only pays on cold climate air-source heat pumps listed on Natural Resources Canada's qualified products list. The list isn't paperwork for its own sake. To get on it, a model has to prove tested performance in cold conditions, so it doubles as a filter against units that call themselves cold climate on the box and quit at -12°C.

Two practical notes: pre-approval has to be in place before installation, so confirm eligibility first, and program terms can change, so we check the list and the current rules on every quote.

What's the best cold climate heat pump for Ontario?

Honest answer: the best cold climate heat pump for Ontario is the one on the NRCan qualified list that's sized correctly for your home's heat loss, matched to the right indoor unit, and installed with the charge, airflow and electrical done properly. A premium model that's oversized will short cycle and underperform a mid-range unit sized right.

Within that, we install the Midea EVOX G3 most often because it combines a -30°C rating, ENERGY STAR cold climate certification and R-454B refrigerant at a price that leaves room for the rebate to matter. Kepler is our pick when quiet operation or a 1.5 ton size is the priority. We run the heat-loss numbers before we quote.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cold climate heat pump?

A cold climate heat pump is an air-source heat pump engineered to deliver most of its rated heating capacity at -15°C and to keep operating down to -25°C or -30°C. It uses an inverter compressor and vapour injection to pull useful heat from very cold air, where a standard heat pump loses output or shuts down.

Do cold climate heat pumps work at -30°C in Ontario?

Yes. The Midea EVOX G3 and Kepler models we install are rated to operate at -30°C and hold strong capacity at -15°C. Output does decline as it gets colder, so for homes in Northern Ontario we add backup heat for the handful of nights that go past the rating.

What is the best cold climate heat pump for Ontario?

The best cold climate heat pump for Ontario is one on NRCan's qualified list that is sized to your home's heat loss and installed correctly. Brand matters less than sizing and installation. We most often install the Midea EVOX G3, with Kepler as the choice for quiet operation or smaller sizes.

Do I still need a furnace or backup heat with a cold climate heat pump?

Many Ontario homes south of Barrie can run on a cold climate heat pump alone with small electric auxiliary strips for defrost and extreme cold. Homes on natural gas usually keep the furnace as a hybrid backup because it is the cheapest way to cover the coldest nights. We size the backup to your region and heat loss.

What does HSPF2 mean on a heat pump?

HSPF2 is Heating Seasonal Performance Factor, version 2: the total heat a heat pump delivers over a heating season divided by the electricity it uses, measured under the current test standard. A higher HSPF2 means lower winter hydro bills. SEER2 is the same idea for cooling.

How much does a cold climate heat pump cost in Ontario?

Cold climate heat pump installation starts at $5,500 installed with EHI. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements such as ductwork, electrical and backup heat, and we give a guaranteed quote before any work. Rebates of up to $7,500 come back after installation for eligible homes.

Does a cold climate heat pump qualify for the Ontario heat pump rebate?

Yes, if the model is on NRCan's qualified products list and pre-approval is in place before installation. Homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood can receive up to $7,500 based on heat pump size, and Enbridge gas-heated homes get a smaller amount, commonly up to $2,000. Confirm eligibility before installation, because program terms can change.
