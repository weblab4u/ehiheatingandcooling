Comfort problems an HVAC technician in Aurora fixes every season

Older systems don't just break down. They make the house less pleasant to live in. These are the complaints families bring us most.

  • Static shocks off every doorknob in February

    Cold outdoor air holds very little moisture, and forced-air heat dries it further. A whole-home humidifier on the furnace brings indoor humidity back to a comfortable level without a bucket humidifier in every bedroom.

  • Dust settles on the furniture a day after you clean

    Leaky return ducts pull dust from the basement and wall cavities. We seal the returns, upgrade the filter cabinet and set the blower to circulate air through a better filter.

  • Windows sweat and the basement smells musty

    That's too much moisture and too little fresh air. An HRV swaps stale indoor air for filtered outdoor air while keeping most of the heat, and an ERV can also help manage moisture.

  • The old furnace bangs when it fires up

    Delayed ignition or a worn blower is the usual cause. On a furnace that's 25 years old, our TSSA-certified gas technicians will tell you if it's a safe repair or a sign to replace.

  • The AC struggles on humid August evenings

    An air conditioner that's 20 years old has lost capacity and probably runs on R-22. A new high-SEER2 AC or a heat pump cools better, pulls out more humidity and costs less to run.

  • The kids' bedrooms are cold while the living room is warm

    Old single-speed blowers can't push enough air to the far rooms. A variable-speed furnace with balanced ducts evens out temperatures across the whole house.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Frequently asked questions

What humidity should my home be at in an Aurora winter?

Most homes are comfortable at about 30% to 40% relative humidity in winter, a little lower during extreme cold to stop condensation on windows. A whole-home humidifier with a humidistat holds that level for you.

What's the difference between an HRV and an ERV?

An HRV brings in fresh air and recovers heat from the stale air going out, while an ERV recovers heat and some moisture. In cold Ontario winters an HRV is the usual pick for tight homes, and an ERV can suit homes that run very dry or very humid.

Can you add a humidifier during furnace installation in Aurora?

Yes, and it's the easiest time to do it. The ductwork and wiring are already open, so the humidifier goes in on the same visit.

Do I need an HRV in an older Aurora home?

Not always. Older, draftier homes often get enough fresh air on their own, but if you've added insulation, new windows or sealed up the basement, an HRV helps clear stale air and moisture.

Is an HVAC technician in Aurora available on Sundays?

Yes. We're open Sunday 10 am to 5 pm and Monday to Saturday 8 am to 8 pm, and we take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays.

Can a heat pump replace my old air conditioner?

Yes, for many homes. A cold-climate heat pump cools like an AC in summer and handles much of the heating too, with your furnace kept as backup for the coldest weather.
