AC repair in Sarnia and other lakeside problems we fix

Living near the water is great until the house feels damp and the outdoor unit takes a beating. Here's what we hear about most.

  • The house feels clammy even with the AC running

    An oversized air conditioner cools the air fast and shuts off before it pulls the moisture out. We size from a heat-gain calculation and favour inverter or variable-speed equipment that runs longer at low speed, which dries the air as it cools.

  • The outdoor unit is rusting well before its time

    Moisture, wind-blown grit and a unit sitting in a puddle all speed up wear. We set equipment on a raised stand or wall bracket, use corrosion-resistant mounting hardware, and rinse the coil at every tune-up.

  • Lake-effect snow buries the heat pump

    A buried coil can't breathe and can't defrost properly. We mount the unit above the usual drift line, on the sheltered side of the house where possible, and leave a clear path for defrost water to drain away.

  • The cottage smells musty when you open it in spring

    Damp air sitting all winter is the usual cause. A ductless heat pump with a dry mode, or an HRV or ERV that swaps stale air for fresh, keeps moisture in check while you're away.

  • Wind off the lake makes the furnace run nonstop

    Wind pulls heat out through gaps and thin walls. We check the furnace size against a real heat-loss number, look for leaky ducts, and recommend two-stage or variable-speed equipment that runs steadily instead of blasting.

  • The AC quits in the middle of a July heat wave

    Failed capacitors, burnt contactors and slow refrigerant leaks top the list. Our licensed refrigeration techs find the fault and give you a written price before any repair.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Air conditioning in Sarnia that handles humidity, not just heat

An air conditioner does two jobs: it lowers the temperature and it pulls water out of the air. In a dry climate the first job matters most. Beside Lake Huron, the second one decides whether you feel comfortable at 23°C or sticky at 21°C.

The fix is not a bigger unit. A bigger unit hits the thermostat setting quickly, shuts off and leaves the moisture behind. A correctly sized inverter AC or heat pump runs longer at lower speed, and that steady run time is what wrings out the humidity.

Placement matters just as much near the water. We keep the outdoor unit off the ground, away from downspouts and out of the strongest wind, with the refrigerant lines sealed and supported so they don't rattle in a storm.

  • Heat-gain sizing that accounts for moisture as well as floor area
  • Inverter and variable-speed options for long, quiet run cycles
  • Raised stands or wall brackets above drift and puddle level
  • Condensate drains checked so water leaves the house cleanly

Frequently asked questions

Why does my Sarnia home feel humid with the AC on?

Most often the air conditioner is too big for the house. It cools quickly and shuts off before it removes enough moisture, so a correctly sized inverter unit usually feels drier at the same temperature.

Where should a heat pump go on a windy lakeside lot?

On the most sheltered side of the house, raised above typical snow drifts, with clear drainage underneath. We check wind direction and roof runoff before choosing the spot.

Do cottages along Lake Huron qualify for heat pump rebates?

It depends on the program terms. A year-round home heated by electricity, oil or propane may qualify for up to $7,500, while a seasonal cottage may not, so we confirm eligibility before installation.

Can a cold-climate heat pump handle lake-effect snow?

Yes, if it's mounted high enough and can drain its defrost water. The units we install are rated to heat down to -30°C.

Do you offer furnace repair in Sarnia?

Yes. Our TSSA-certified gas technicians repair gas furnaces and give you a written price before starting.

Is emergency HVAC in Sarnia available after hours?

Yes. We take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays. Call 1-647-694-5845 and we'll tell you when a technician can get there.

How often should a lakeside air conditioner be serviced?

Once a year, ideally in spring. We clean and rinse the coil, check the refrigerant charge and electrical parts, and look for early corrosion.
