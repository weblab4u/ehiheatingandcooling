Heat pump installation in Parry Sound when there's no gas line

When natural gas isn't an option, you're choosing between fuels you have delivered and electricity. A cold-climate heat pump tips that choice. It runs on hydro but moves heat rather than making it, so it gets far more warmth out of each kilowatt-hour than baseboards, and it does away with most of the oil or propane bill.

The rebate side is where off-gas homes come out ahead. Homes without natural gas sit in the top bracket of the Home Renovation Savings Program, as much as $7,500 toward an eligible cold-climate heat pump, while gas-heated homes get less. Only models on NRCan's qualified list count, and the pre-approval has to come through before the install. We check that you qualify first, then file the forms for you.

Getting the equipment there is its own puzzle on the bay. Granite outcrops, steep driveways and seasonal roads all change how we plan install day, so we sort that out on the site visit, not the morning of.

  • EVOX G3 and Kepler heat pumps with a -30°C heating rating
  • Backup heat kept or added for the coldest nights
  • Outdoor unit on a bracket or a stand pinned to rock
  • A dedicated 240V circuit, run as ESA-licensed electrical work

Off-gas heating problems a Parry Sound HVAC contractor fixes

No gas line means a different set of headaches. Around the Sound, these come up all the time.

  • Keeping the wood stove going is a full-time job

    A heat pump holds the temperature on its own, day and night. Keep the stove for the evenings you want it, and stop getting up at 3 am to feed it.

  • The oil tank in the basement makes you nervous

    After the switch, the oil burner barely runs. Leave it as a fallback for a winter if you like, then book a licensed fuel oil contractor to take the tank out when it suits you.

  • Baseboard heat in a waterfront home costs a fortune by March

    A ductless heat pump gives you the same warmth for a fraction of the hydro, and electrically heated homes usually get the bigger rebate. Leave the baseboards on the wall as a fallback.

  • The propane company's delivery schedule runs your winter

    Hand the everyday heating to a heat pump and the propane furnace turns into a cold-night helper. The gauge on the tank barely moves for weeks.

  • Your lot is all rock and slope, with nowhere flat for a unit

    Rock is actually a solid base. We bolt a stand into it or hang the unit on the wall, high enough to clear the snow, with a drain path that won't freeze into a sheet across the step.

  • Wind off Georgian Bay finds every gap in the house

    A point or an open shoreline takes more wind than a sheltered lot in town, so we work exposure and air leakage into the heat-loss numbers before choosing a size.

  • Finding a crew willing to come out this far is a struggle

    We plan Parry Sound work in advance, with the equipment, parts and crew lined up before we set out, so the job gets done in as few trips as possible.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Frequently asked questions

Do homes around Parry Sound qualify for the $7,500 heat pump rebate?

Many do, because they heat with oil, propane, wood or electricity rather than natural gas. The exact figure follows the heat pump's size, only NRCan-listed models count, and you need pre-approval before the install.

Can I keep my wood stove after installing a heat pump?

Yes. Plenty of owners keep the stove for cozy evenings or as backup during a power outage, while the heat pump handles everyday heating.

How do you install a heat pump on a rocky Georgian Bay lot?

Granite makes a firm anchor, so we often fix a stand right to it or use a wall mount. Either way the unit sits above the usual snow and out from under the eaves, in a spot we agree on during the visit.

Can you install at a water-access cottage near Parry Sound?

Sometimes. Tell us how the property is reached, and we'll talk through whether the equipment and crew can get there safely and what that adds to the plan.

Do you offer HVAC services in Parry Sound for oil furnaces?

Yes. We replace oil furnaces with cold-climate heat pumps, or pair a heat pump with the existing furnace as backup, for homes in Parry Sound, Seguin, Carling and McDougall.

Is a heat pump enough on its own for a Georgian Bay winter?

With the right size and a backup source, yes. We plan backup heat for every home on the bay, using your current furnace, baseboards or an electric air handler.
