Air conditioning problems a new central AC fixes

Most air conditioner replacement calls start with one of these. Here is what is usually behind it, and what we do.

  • Your AC is 15 years old and the repair quote is scary

    Once a compressor or evaporator coil fails on an older unit, the repair often costs a third of a new system. We give you both numbers, repair and replace, so you can decide with real figures. See our AC repair and maintenance page if you want to try a fix first.

  • The upstairs bedrooms never cool down

    Nine times out of ten the old unit was sized off square footage, or the ductwork starves the second floor. We run a heat-gain calculation, check static pressure in the ducts, and often recommend a two-stage or variable-speed system that runs longer at low speed and pushes cool air where it is needed.

  • Your hydro bill jumps every summer

    A unit from the early 2000s can use far more electricity per hour of cooling than a current model. SEER2 is the rating that matters. The higher the number, the less hydro you burn for the same cooling.

  • The outdoor unit rattles loud enough to bother the neighbours

    Worn compressor mounts and tired fan motors get loud. New Lennox and Midea condensers run quietly enough that you forget they are there, which your neighbours will appreciate too.

  • The AC died in a heat wave and you have no cooling

    We carry common sizes and can often replace a central air conditioner within days, not weeks. Call 1-647-694-5845 and we will give you the soonest install date.

  • You are wondering if a heat pump makes more sense

    It often does. A cold-climate heat pump cools exactly like an AC and also heats your home, and a qualifying model can bring back up to $7,500 through the Home Renovation Savings Program. Read about heat pump installation before you buy a plain AC.

  • Someone quoted you a new condenser without touching the furnace coil

    The evaporator coil inside your furnace must match the new outdoor unit, or you lose efficiency and risk the warranty. Every EHI air conditioner installation includes a matched coil and a new line set where needed.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

What is included in an EHI air conditioner installation

  • Heat-gain calculation (Manual J style) using your square footage, windows, insulation and sun exposure
  • Recovery of the old refrigerant and removal of the old condenser and coil by licensed refrigeration technicians
  • New matched evaporator coil installed on your furnace or air handler
  • New insulated copper line set, or a pressure test of the existing one if it is the right size and clean
  • Level composite pad or wall bracket, with clearance for airflow and snow
  • 240V circuit, disconnect and whip checked or replaced by ESA-licensed electricians
  • Vacuum to 500 microns, refrigerant charge weighed in, and a condensate drain with trap
  • Thermostat set-up, permits where required, and warranty registration with the manufacturer

SEER2, R-454B and the matched coil: what actually decides how your AC performs

SEER2 is the seasonal efficiency rating every new air conditioner sold in Canada carries. It replaced SEER with a tougher test that better reflects real ductwork. A higher SEER2 means lower hydro bills for the same cooling.

R-454B is the low-global-warming-potential refrigerant in the current generation of equipment, including the Midea EVOX G3. It needs properly rated line sets and tools, which is why licensed refrigeration techs should do the work.

The matched coil is the part most homeowners never see. The evaporator coil inside your furnace has to be sized and rated for the new outdoor unit. Mismatched coils are the most common reason a brand-new AC underperforms.

Frequently asked questions

How much does central air conditioner installation cost in Ontario?

Central air conditioner installation cost in Ontario depends on tonnage, efficiency level, and whether your line set, coil and electrical can be reused, so EHI quotes each home with a guaranteed final price. As a comparison, a cold-climate heat pump that cools like an AC and also heats starts at $5,500 installed, and may qualify for a rebate that a plain AC does not.

What size air conditioner do I need?

The right size comes from a heat-gain calculation, not square footage alone. As a rough guide, many Ontario homes up to about 2,000 square feet land in the 2 to 3 ton range, and larger two-storey homes often need 3.5 to 5 ton. An oversized AC short cycles and leaves the house clammy, so we measure before we recommend.

Should I replace my AC with a heat pump?

For most Ontario homes with ductwork, yes, it is worth a serious look. A cold-climate heat pump provides the same cooling as a central AC, heats your home for most of the winter, and qualifying models can return up to $7,500 through the Home Renovation Savings Program. We will quote both options side by side.

How long does AC installation take?

A straight central air conditioner replacement usually takes four to six hours. A first-time installation with a new line set, coil and 240V circuit takes most of one day.

What does SEER2 mean on a new air conditioner?

SEER2 is the seasonal energy efficiency ratio measured under the updated test procedure that reflects real-world ductwork. A higher SEER2 number means the unit delivers more cooling per kilowatt-hour, which shows up as a lower summer hydro bill.

Do I need a new coil on my furnace when I replace the AC?

Yes, in almost every case. The evaporator coil must match the capacity and refrigerant of the new outdoor unit, or the system will not reach its rated efficiency and the manufacturer may not honour the warranty. EHI includes a matched coil in every air conditioner replacement.

Can you install an air conditioner in a house with no ducts?

Yes. A ductless mini-split or multi-zone system cools and heats rooms without any ductwork, which suits bungalows with baseboard heat, additions and condos. See our ductless mini-split installation page for options and pricing.
