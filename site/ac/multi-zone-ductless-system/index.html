Problems a multi zone ductless system solves

These are the homes where a multi-zone system beats every other option.

  • No ducts, and electric baseboard bills that hurt

    A multi-zone heat pump delivers two to three units of heat for every unit of electricity, even on cold days. For an all-electric home this is the biggest bill cut available, and it qualifies for the higher rebate tier.

  • Every bedroom has a window AC and the hydro meter is spinning

    Replace them all with one quiet outdoor unit and a head in each room. Better efficiency, real heating in winter, and the windows open again.

  • One thermostat, and the family fights over it

    Each head has its own control. The office at 21°C, the bedroom at 18°C, the basement off when nobody is down there. Nobody argues with a remote.

  • A single-zone head is not reaching the closed-off rooms

    Open-concept areas do fine with one head. Bedrooms with doors closed do not. A multi-zone system puts conditioned air where the doors are.

  • Hot water radiators or in-floor heat, but no summer cooling

    Keep the boiler for winter if you like, and add a multi-zone system for cooling. Many homeowners end up using the heat pump for shoulder seasons too, because it is cheaper to run.

  • You do not want wall units in every room

    Mix and match. Wall-mounted heads in bedrooms, a ceiling cassette in the living room, and a slim-duct head hidden in a bulkhead serving two small rooms through short duct runs.

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Multi-zone capacities and the homes they fit

Midea Extreme Hyper Heat multi-zone outdoor units come in five sizes. The right one depends on a heat-loss calculation for each zone, not just head count, but this gives you a starting point.

Outdoor unitIndoor headsTypical home
18,000 BTU2 headsTwo bedrooms, or a main floor plus one bedroom in a small bungalow or condo
27,000 BTU2 to 3 headsBungalow: living area plus two bedrooms
36,000 BTU3 to 4 headsThree-bedroom bungalow or small two-storey
48,000 BTU4 to 5 headsTwo-storey home: living area, three bedrooms, basement
60,000 BTUUp to 5 headsLarger two-storey or full whole-home ductless replacement

Heads are sized to each room. A 12,000 BTU wall unit in a small bedroom will short cycle, so we match each head to its room rather than loading up on capacity.

Indoor head styles you can mix on one system

Every head on a multi-zone system can be a different style. Pick by room, not by system.

Wall-mounted heads

The standard choice. Mounted high on a wall, quiet, with a washable filter and a remote. Best for bedrooms, offices and living rooms.

Ceiling cassettes

Recessed into the ceiling with four-way airflow. Nearly invisible, good for larger living areas and kitchens where wall space is tight.

Slim-duct (concealed) heads

Hidden in a bulkhead or closet, feeding two or three small rooms through short ducts and discreet grilles. Great for upstairs bedrooms that share a hallway.

Need a single zone instead?

If only one room is the problem, a single-zone Midea Hyper Heat unit from $5,500 is the simpler answer.

What is included in every EHI multi-zone installation

  • Room-by-room heat-loss and heat-gain calculation to size each head and the outdoor unit
  • Indoor heads mounted and levelled, with sealed wall sleeves and condensate drains or pumps
  • Outdoor unit on a riser stand or wall bracket above the snow line, with proper clearances
  • Individual insulated line sets and control wiring to each head, in a tidy line set cover
  • Dedicated 240V circuit and disconnect by ESA-licensed electricians, with a panel capacity check
  • Nitrogen pressure test, vacuum to 500 microns and refrigerant charge trimmed for the total line length
  • Commissioning of every zone in heating, cooling and defrost, plus remote and app set-up
  • Warranty registration and rebate pre-approval and submission where the system qualifies

Frequently asked questions

How much does a multi zone mini split cost in Ontario?

Multi-zone ductless systems in Ontario start at $5,500 installed with EHI, and the final price depends on the outdoor unit size, the number and style of heads and how far the line sets run. We quote a guaranteed final price after a room-by-room design visit, before any work begins.

How many rooms can a multi zone heat pump handle?

A Midea Extreme Hyper Heat multi-zone system runs two to five indoor heads from one outdoor unit, with outdoor capacities from 18,000 to 60,000 BTU. A slim-duct head can serve two or three small rooms, so five heads can cover a typical two-storey home.

Can a multi zone ductless system heat a whole house in winter?

Yes, when it is sized from a heat-loss calculation. Midea Extreme Hyper Heat multi-zone units are rated to -30°C and keep strong capacity at -15°C. Homes in Northern Ontario sometimes keep baseboards or a furnace as backup for the coldest nights.

Can I mix wall units and ceiling cassettes on the same system?

Yes. Wall-mounted, ceiling cassette and slim-duct heads can all run on the same Midea multi-zone outdoor unit, so each room gets the style that suits it.

Do multi-zone systems qualify for the Ontario heat pump rebate?

Cold-climate multi-zone ductless heat pumps can qualify for the Home Renovation Savings Program when the model is on NRCan's qualified products list and pre-approval is completed before installation. The amount depends on system size and your current heating fuel, and we handle the application.

Is a multi-zone system better than a central heat pump?

If your home has ductwork in good shape, a central heat pump is usually simpler and lower cost. If there are no ducts, or you want room-by-room control, multi-zone ductless is the better fit. We will tell you which one makes sense for your house.
