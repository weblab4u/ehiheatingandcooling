What bothers Cobourg homeowners about their old systems

Most people don't call us because the heat is off. They call because something has been nagging at them for years.

  • The furnace roars to life and wakes you at night

    A single-stage furnace has one speed, full blast, every time it fires. A two-stage, variable-speed furnace or an inverter heat pump starts low and ramps up gently, so you barely notice it running.

  • Upstairs rooms in an older house swing between chilly and stuffy

    Many homes near downtown were built before forced air, and the upper floor gets little airflow. A ductless head or two upstairs gives those rooms their own heating and cooling without opening walls.

  • Wind off the lake finds every draft in the front rooms

    We look for air leaks first, then size heat room by room. A ductless head on the exposed side, or a better-balanced duct system, evens the house out.

  • A loud outdoor unit sits right under the bedroom window

    We place outdoor units away from bedrooms and neighbours where the lot allows, mount them on anti-vibration pads or wall brackets, and choose inverter models that run quietly at low speed.

  • Fixed income, rising bills and an old system you can't predict

    We give you one guaranteed price, show the rebate you may qualify for and lay out 0% financing for 3, 6 or 9 months. You decide when and how, with no pressure.

  • Window air conditioners are getting too heavy to haul out every spring

    A ductless heat pump stays on the wall all year, cools in summer, heats in winter and runs from a remote. No more lifting units in and out of windows.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Ductless heat pump installation in Cobourg's heritage homes

A ductless heat pump has two parts: a slim indoor head mounted high on a wall and a compact outdoor unit, joined by a small refrigerant line through the wall. It heats in winter and cools in summer, and it does both quietly because the inverter compressor speeds up and slows down instead of slamming on and off.

That suits older houses near downtown and West Harbour, where radiators or baseboards do the heating and there are no ducts to carry central air. Two or three heads usually cover the main floor and bedrooms, and one multi-zone outdoor unit runs them all with separate controls.

Because Cobourg sits right on the lake, winters are a little milder than inland, so a cold-climate heat pump spends much of the season in its most efficient range. We still size it from a room-by-room heat loss, and you can keep your existing heat as backup.

  • Heads placed where they're easy to clean and out of the way
  • Line sets hidden in covers you can paint to match the house
  • Outdoor unit on a stand or bracket, clear of snow and away from bedrooms
  • Simple remote or thermostat control, set up and explained

Frequently asked questions

How quiet is a ductless heat pump in a Cobourg home?

Indoors it's usually a soft hum at low speed, because inverter models ramp up and down instead of cycling hard on and off. We place the outdoor unit away from bedrooms and neighbours wherever the lot allows.

Will a heat pump work in an older heritage house near downtown Cobourg?

Yes. Ductless systems need only a small opening for the refrigerant lines, so we can heat and cool older homes without adding ductwork, and we pick discreet spots for the outdoor unit.

What financing is available for HVAC in Cobourg?

You can choose 0% interest over 3, 6 or 9 months, or an open loan starting at 2.99% OAC. Rates are subject to lender approval, and we show you the options alongside your quote.

Do Cobourg homes qualify for the heat pump rebate?

Many do. Homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood can qualify for up to $7,500 toward a cold-climate heat pump, and Enbridge gas-heated homes usually get a smaller amount. Pre-approval is needed before installation, and we handle the paperwork.

Is it worth replacing a furnace that still works?

Sometimes. If it's old, loud or costly to run, a high-efficiency furnace or a heat pump can pay off, especially with rebates for eligible homes, but if it's sound and quiet, a tune-up may be all you need.

Will you come out to Grafton or Baltimore, or only Cobourg itself?

Yes. Our crews serve Cobourg, Grafton, Baltimore and the rest of Hamilton Township, and we set visit times with you.
