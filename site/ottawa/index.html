What an Ottawa winter asks of your heating

Ottawa is one of the coldest big cities in the country, and its summers still get hot and sticky. One system has to handle both ends.

  • ClimateRegular -25°C nights

    Long winters with deep cold snaps mean the system has to deliver full heat at the bottom of the thermometer, not just on a mild November day.

  • UtilitiesHydro Ottawa and Enbridge Gas

    Most homes have both. We compare your hydro rate against your gas bill before we suggest where a heat pump should hand off to the furnace.

  • HousingGlebe brick to Kanata two-storeys

    Older homes in the Glebe and Alta Vista lose heat differently than newer builds in Barrhaven or Orléans, so the sizing changes house to house.

  • RebatesUp to $7,500

    Homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood can reach the top tier. Gas-heated homes qualify for a smaller amount, commonly up to $2,000.

Homes we work with as an HVAC contractor in Ottawa

Most of the Ottawa homeowners who call us fall into one of these four groups. Each gets a different plan.

Suburban homes on natural gas

Plenty of houses in Kanata, Nepean and Orléans have a working gas furnace and an aging AC. Swapping the AC for a cold-climate heat pump lets it carry most of the winter, and the furnace takes over only in the deepest cold.

Older homes inside the Greenbelt

Brick and frame houses in Westboro and the Glebe often have small ducts or none upstairs. Ductless heads or a slim ducted unit can heat and cool those rooms without tearing into plaster.

Homes on baseboards or oil

If you heat with electric baseboards, oil or propane, a heat pump can cut your heating costs sharply and usually lands you in the higher rebate tier.

AC that's ready to retire

A heat pump cools just like a central air conditioner on a humid July night. Replacing a tired AC with one gives you two jobs for the price of one outdoor unit.

Where Ottawa HVAC systems fall short, and how we fix them

These are the complaints we hear most once the snow sets in. None of them is something you have to live with.

  • Your heat pump blows lukewarm air once it drops below -15°C

    Standard heat pumps lose most of their output in hard cold. A cold-climate model like the Midea EVOX G3 is rated to -30°C and holds strong capacity at -15°C, so it keeps working through a normal Ottawa January.

  • The furnace runs nonstop and the house still feels cool

    That usually points to an undersized unit, leaky ducts or a clogged filter. We measure static pressure, check the ductwork and size any replacement from a proper heat-loss calculation, then fit a two-stage, variable-speed furnace if that's the right call.

  • Someone sized your last system by square footage

    Rules of thumb oversize in some homes and undersize in others. We do a room-by-room heat-loss calculation that accounts for windows, insulation and air leakage, so the equipment matches the house you actually live in.

  • Upstairs bedrooms in your two-storey never warm up

    Common in the big Barrhaven and Stittsville builds. We look at return air, duct sizing and balancing first, and add a ductless head for a problem room when the ducts can't carry enough heat.

  • The outdoor unit disappears under a snowbank by February

    We mount it on a raised stand or wall bracket above typical snow depth, away from the roof drip line, with a clear path for defrost water to drain so it doesn't freeze into a block.

  • You want lower gas bills without giving up the furnace

    A hybrid system runs the heat pump for most of the season and switches to gas when it gets bitterly cold. We set the switchover point from your actual hydro and gas rates, not a factory default.

  • Freezing rain has glazed the outdoor unit and the fan sounds wrong

    Ottawa gets ice storms that a Toronto unit never sees. Don't chip at it. Switch the system to backup heat, let the defrost cycle and the sun do the work, and call us if the fan still rubs once the ice is gone. On the install side, we keep the unit out from under eaves and gutters, where meltwater refreezes on the coil, and leave room above the fan for ice to clear.

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Frequently asked questions

Will a heat pump keep my Ottawa home warm at -30°C?

A cold-climate heat pump like the Midea EVOX G3 is rated to heat down to -30°C, so it can carry most Ottawa homes through a normal winter. For the coldest nights we pair it with backup heat, either your gas furnace or an electric air handler, sized from a heat-loss calculation.

Should I keep my gas furnace when I add a heat pump?

In most Ottawa homes with a working gas furnace, yes. A hybrid setup lets the heat pump do the bulk of the heating and hands off to the furnace in deep cold, which keeps comfort steady and the bills reasonable.

How much is the heat pump rebate for an Ottawa home on natural gas?

Enbridge gas-heated homes commonly qualify for up to $2,000 through the Home Renovation Savings Program, while homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood can reach up to $7,500. You need pre-approval before the install, and the program's terms can change, so we confirm eligibility first.

What is a heat-loss calculation?

A heat-loss calculation is a room-by-room estimate of how much heat your home loses on a very cold day. It tells us what size heat pump or furnace you need, so the system is neither too small for January nor so big that it short cycles.

Is EHI an HVAC company in Ottawa?

Our office is in Stouffville, and our licensed crews serve Ottawa and the surrounding area. We schedule assessments and installs with you directly and give you a written, guaranteed quote.

Do you offer emergency HVAC in Ottawa?

Yes, we take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays. Call 1-647-694-5845 and we'll tell you honestly when a technician can get to you.

Does a heat pump also handle Ottawa's humid summers?

Yes. A heat pump cools and dehumidifies like a central air conditioner, so one outdoor unit covers both the July humidity and the January cold.

How much is a cold-climate heat pump installed in Ottawa?

Cold-climate heat pumps start from $5,500 installed, before the Home Renovation Savings Program rebate. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, including the backup heat and any 240V circuit, and your written quote is guaranteed.
