When northern Muskoka cold tests your heating

Huntsville winters find the weak spot in any system. These are the ones we fix most often.

  • The lakefront living room never feels warm in a north wind

    Big windows facing open water lose heat fast, and wind pushes cold air through every gap. We size the system from a heat-loss calculation that counts glass area and exposure, then place heads or registers to cover those walls.

  • Your heat pump runs nonstop on the coldest mornings

    On the deepest cold days, steady running is normal for an inverter heat pump. If the house still loses ground, the backup isn't set right or the unit is undersized. We check both and adjust the switchover.

  • The bunkie is too cold for guests past September

    A small single-zone ductless heat pump heats a bunkie through the shoulder seasons and cools it in July. It needs a 240V circuit and a short line set, and no ductwork at all.

  • The new addition is on its own heating island

    Stretching old ducts to an addition rarely works well. A dedicated ductless head gives that space its own heat, cooling and thermostat.

  • The outdoor unit sits under a wall of snow by February

    We set it on a tall stand or a wall bracket, well above the depth your yard usually sees, and away from the eaves so falling snow and ice miss it.

  • Propane or oil costs keep climbing at the lake house

    A cold-climate heat pump takes most of the load and leaves the furnace as backup. If the lake house runs on propane, oil, wood or electric heat, it's in line for the larger rebate.

  • The cottage road isn't plowed and you close the place up in November

    A heat pump can sit idle through winter, but it should be shut down properly. We show you how to switch it off at the disconnect, clear the condensate line so nothing freezes and splits, and leave the outdoor unit covered against snow load without blocking it. In spring, a quick check before the first run catches anything the winter did.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Heat pump installation in Huntsville with backup heat built in

A heat pump's rating tells you the coldest temperature it can still produce heat at. Our Midea EVOX G3 and Kepler units carry a -30°C heating rating. In northern Muskoka, some winters bring nights near or past that, so a rating alone isn't a plan.

The plan is a heat pump sized to carry most of the season, plus a backup sized to carry the whole house if it has to. In a home with a good furnace, the furnace becomes the backup. In an all-electric home, an electric air handler with heat strips does the job. Either way, the switch happens on its own and you stay warm.

Bunkies and additions are simpler. A single-zone ductless system handles one space, and the main house's system doesn't have to change at all.

  • Output checked at your design temperature, not just the label
  • A second heat source sized for the full load
  • Separate ductless zones for bunkies and additions
  • A clear path for defrost water, so no ice block forms below

Frequently asked questions

Can a heat pump keep a Huntsville lake house warm at -30°C?

It will still be making heat at -30°C, but up here we never let it work without a safety net. On the coldest nights the backup takes over automatically, so the house stays warm.

What size heat pump does a bunkie need?

Most bunkies need a small single-zone ductless unit, but the right size depends on the floor area, insulation and windows. We size it from a quick heat-loss check rather than a guess.

Does a bunkie heat pump need its own electrical circuit?

Yes. A ductless heat pump needs a dedicated 240V circuit, and we check whether your panel or the bunkie's sub-panel has room for it.

What backup heat works best in Huntsville?

A furnace that still has years left makes an easy backup in a hybrid setup. Homes with no furnace worth keeping usually go with an electric air handler that has heat strips inside.

Do you install heat pumps on Peninsula and Fairy Lake properties?

Yes. We install at lakefront homes and cottages around Huntsville, and we plan placement around wind, snow and access on the site visit.

Is there an HVAC contractor in Huntsville for oil-to-heat-pump switches?

We handle oil-to-heat-pump switches for Huntsville homes from our base in Stouffville. If your home burns oil now, it may qualify for up to $7,500, provided pre-approval comes before the install.

What does a heat pump cost for a Huntsville lake house?

A ducted cold-climate heat pump starts from $5,500 installed, and a single-zone ductless unit for a bunkie or addition is quoted in writing after we see the space. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, especially the line-set run and the backup heat, and the quote is guaranteed.
