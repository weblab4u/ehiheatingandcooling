Leaving oil and propane behind in Brock Township

The Home Renovation Savings Program pays more when a heat pump replaces oil, propane, electric or wood heat. That describes a large share of homes around Beaverton, Cannington and Sunderland, where the gas line doesn't always reach. Qualifying cold-climate heat pumps can earn up to $7,500, based on the size of the system.

The rebate is only half the story. Oil and propane arrive by truck and are priced by the litre, and baseboards turn each kilowatt into exactly one kilowatt of heat. A heat pump moves heat from the outdoor air instead, so for many homes the running cost drops once it takes over.

There are rules. The heat pump must be on NRCan's qualified products list, and pre-approval has to be in place before installation. Before anything else, we check that your home and the equipment qualify, and then we do the paperwork. Program terms can change, so we check the current rules for every job.

  • Qualifying cold-climate equipment from Midea and Kepler
  • Pre-approval filed before install day

Problems a Beaverton HVAC technician hears about every winter

Lakeside, village or farm, the complaints sound alike. So do the fixes.

  • Baseboard heat leaves the hydro bill high and the floors cold

    Baseboards warm the air near the wall and little else. A ductless or ducted heat pump spreads heat more evenly and uses much less electricity for the same warmth on most winter days.

  • The oil tank gauge drops faster than you'd like

    Oil furnaces in older homes can burn through a fill in one cold stretch. A cold-climate heat pump can take over most of the season, and the oil furnace can stay as backup until you're ready to retire it.

  • Wind off Lake Simcoe chills the lake side of the house

    Strong shoreline wind pulls heat out of exposed walls. We size from a heat-loss calculation that accounts for that exposure, then add a ductless head where one side of the house lags.

  • A converted cottage runs on a patchwork of heaters

    Space heaters, a wood stove and a couple of baseboards rarely add up to even heat. A multi-zone ductless system replaces the patchwork with one outdoor unit and a head in each main room, each controlled on its own.

  • The farmhouse furnace is too old to trust

    An ageing propane or oil furnace on a farm near Sunderland or Cannington is a good reason to look at a heat pump with an electric air handler. It uses the ducts you have and qualifies for the higher rebate tier when the equipment is on the qualified list.

  • You'd like cooling for muggy July nights too

    A heat pump is also an air conditioner. The same system that heats in January cools and dehumidifies in summer, so there's no separate AC to buy.

  • The place at Port Bolster runs on a wood stove and a lot of optimism

    Wood heat is fine until you're away for three days in January. A ductless heat pump becomes the main heat, the stove stays for the evenings you want it, and the house holds temperature on its own. Homes heated mainly with wood are in the higher rebate tier too, which many owners don't realize until we go through the form with them.

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Frequently asked questions

How much heat pump rebate can a Beaverton home get?

Homes heated with oil, propane, electricity or wood can qualify for up to $7,500 toward a qualifying cold-climate heat pump, based on its size. Pre-approval comes first, before any installation work.

Will a heat pump keep up with Lake Simcoe winters?

Yes, when it's a cold-climate model sized properly. Units like the Midea EVOX G3 are rated to heat down to -30°C, and we plan backup heat for the coldest nights.

Can an HVAC technician in Beaverton replace my baseboards with a heat pump?

Yes. Homes without ducts usually get a ductless or multi-zone heat pump, and many baseboard-heated homes qualify for the higher rebate tier.

Is heat pump installation in Beaverton possible at a cottage?

Yes. Ductless heat pumps suit lakeside cottages and year-round homes, and they cool in summer too.

Does a Beaverton homeowner need an energy audit for the heat pump rebate?

No. A heat pump on its own doesn't require a home energy assessment under the Home Renovation Savings Program, but the model must be on NRCan's qualified list and pre-approved before install.

Do you cover Cannington and Sunderland?

Yes. We serve Beaverton, Cannington, Sunderland, Gamebridge, Port Bolster and the rest of Brock Township.

What does it cost to replace oil or propane heat with a heat pump in Beaverton?

A ducted cold-climate heat pump starts from $5,500 installed, before a rebate of up to $7,500 for homes heated by oil, propane, electricity or wood. An electric air handler to replace the furnace, and any panel work, go on the same written quote. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, and the quote is guaranteed.
