Why one floor is always the wrong temperature

Big two-storey houses share a handful of comfort problems. Here's what causes each one and how we sort it out.

  • Bedrooms upstairs feel like a sauna by 9 pm

    Heat rises, the attic cooks the ceilings, and a single-stage AC shuts off as soon as the hallway thermostat is happy. A variable-speed system runs longer at a lower speed, which pulls more heat and humidity out of the upper floor.

  • The basement feels like a cellar even with the furnace running

    Subdivision homes often have too few supply registers in the lower level. We check static pressure, adjust the dampers, and where it makes sense add a supply run or a ductless head so the basement stops lagging behind.

  • The furnace roars on, blasts hot air, then cuts out

    That's a single-stage furnace doing exactly what it was built to do. A two-stage, variable-speed model like the Lennox ML296V spends most of its time on low fire, so heat spreads evenly and the noise drops.

  • One thermostat can't please the whole family

    Zoning splits the house into areas with their own thermostats, using motorized dampers in the ducts. It works best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, sized together so the system stays quiet when only one zone is calling.

  • Gas bills keep climbing and the house still isn't comfortable

    A hybrid system adds a cold-climate heat pump to your furnace. The heat pump covers most of the heating season and the furnace steps in during deep cold, so you burn less gas without giving up backup heat.

  • Your heritage home in Unionville has nowhere to run ducts

    Older houses in Unionville and Markham Village often have plaster walls and tight framing. Ductless and multi-zone systems add heating and cooling with one small wall opening per indoor head.

  • The Cornell townhouse keeps its furnace in a closet and the AC on a tiny rear pad

    Tight townhome mechanical rooms limit what fits, and the outdoor unit often sits a metre from a neighbour's bedroom window. We measure clearances before choosing a model, pick a compact furnace with the right venting route, and use a quiet inverter outdoor unit on a bracket or slim pad so it stays within the space and the noise stays down.

What a good HVAC company in Markham checks in a two-storey house

A two-storey house is really two climates stacked on top of each other. In summer the upper floor soaks up heat from the roof and the sun on the windows. In winter the basement sits against cold ground. One thermostat in the front hall can't read both.

So before we quote, we look at the whole house. We measure static pressure on the ducts, count supply and return registers on each floor, and run a Manual J heat-loss and heat-gain calculation. Then we pick equipment that fits the house, not whatever happens to be on the truck.

  • Supply and return balance on every floor
  • Duct leaks at the trunk line and basement joints
  • Furnace and AC size compared with the actual load
  • Whether zoning, a variable-speed blower or one ductless head solves it for less
  • Panel space for a 240V heat pump circuit

Frequently asked questions

Why is the upstairs of my Markham house so hot?

Heat rises, the roof warms the top floor, and most homes have one thermostat on the main level. Better return airflow, a variable-speed blower, zoning or a ductless head upstairs usually solves it.

Is zoning worth it in a two-storey home?

Zoning is worth it when floors stay several degrees apart even after the ducts are balanced. It works best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment.

What is a hybrid heat pump system?

A hybrid heat pump system is a cold-climate heat pump paired with a gas furnace, where the heat pump handles most heating and all cooling and the furnace takes over in deep cold. One thermostat controls the switch.

How much does heat pump installation in Markham cost?

Cold-climate heat pumps start from $5,500 installed, and high-efficiency furnaces start from $3,300. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, and every quote is guaranteed in writing.

Do Markham HVAC companies need permits to replace a furnace?

Most furnace and heat pump installs need a permit, and all gas work must be done by TSSA-certified technicians. We look after the permits that apply to your job.

Can you add heating and cooling to a heritage home in Unionville?

Yes. In older Unionville and Markham Village homes we often use ductless or multi-zone systems, which need small wall openings instead of new duct runs.

How many zones does a two-storey Markham home usually need?

Two is the most common: one for the main floor and basement, one for the upstairs bedrooms. Larger homes sometimes add a third for a bonus room or a finished basement. The number comes from how the ducts are laid out and which areas drift apart, which we check before quoting.
