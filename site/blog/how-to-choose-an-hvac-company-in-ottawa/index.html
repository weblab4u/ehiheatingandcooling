How to Choose an HVAC Company in Ottawa

Published October 2026 · Written by the EHI Heating And Cooling team · Reviewed against current TSSA, ESA and Home Renovation Savings Program rules

A good HVAC company in Ottawa holds the right licences, sizes equipment from a heat-loss calculation instead of a chart, puts its price in writing and knows how to get you the rebate. This guide shows you how to check each of those before you sign, what the work should cost, which red flags to walk away from, and how Ottawa's cold and its housing stock change the advice. There's a checklist at the end to bring to every quote.

What a licensed HVAC company in Ottawa must have

Heating and cooling work in Ontario touches gas, refrigerant and electricity. Each one has its own regulator, and a legitimate company in Ottawa can show you all of them without fuss.

TSSA registration for gas work. Any company that installs or repairs a gas furnace, water heater or hybrid system must be registered with the Technical Standards and Safety Authority as a fuels contractor, and the person doing the work must hold a TSSA gas technician certificate. Ask the technician to show the card. It carries their name and certificate class, and a real tech won't blink at the question.

Licensed refrigeration technicians. Anyone connecting a heat pump, central AC or ductless system handles refrigerant. In Ontario that calls for a refrigeration and air conditioning mechanic licence through Skilled Trades Ontario and an Ozone Depletion Prevention card. A company that sends an unlicensed helper to braze your line set is gambling with your warranty and with the refrigerant.

ESA licence for electrical work. A heat pump needs a dedicated 240V circuit, and sometimes a panel upgrade. That work must be done by a Licensed Electrical Contractor with an ECRA/ESA licence number, and a notification must be filed with the Electrical Safety Authority. You can confirm any licence number on ESA's public contractor lookup. If the HVAC company doesn't hold the licence itself, ask which electrical contractor it uses and check that one instead.

WSIB coverage and liability insurance. Ask for a current WSIB clearance certificate and a certificate of liability insurance. If someone is hurt on your property or a line set floods your basement, these two documents decide who pays.

None of this takes long. A company that does the work properly will have all of it ready and won't mind being asked.

The 7 questions to ask before you sign

Ask every company the same seven questions and write the answers down. The differences will tell you more than the price does.

  1. Can I have the quote in writing, with model numbers? "Heat pump, 3 ton" tells you nothing. The quote should name the outdoor unit, the indoor coil or air handler, the thermostat and anything else going in, so you can look up the ratings and compare like with like.
  2. Who actually does the install? Your own employees, or subcontractors? If subs, who holds the licences, and who do I call when something goes wrong a year from now?
  3. What permits or notifications are needed, and who files them? For a new circuit, an ESA notification. For gas, the company's TSSA registration covers the work, but ask anyway. The answer "you don't need any" for a heat pump install is wrong.
  4. How did you size this system? The answer you want is a room-by-room heat-loss calculation using Ottawa's design temperature. "Same size as the old one" or "by square footage" is not an answer, and in this city it leads to a heat pump that falls behind in January.
  5. Who registers the warranty, and what does it cover? Parts, labour, and for how long. Many extended warranties require registration within a set window after install and annual maintenance to stay valid. Get the registration confirmation in your own inbox.
  6. Is my rebate pre-approved before install day? The Home Renovation Savings Program requires pre-approval before installation. A company that says "we'll sort it out after" has just cost you the rebate.
  7. What happens if the price changes once you're in the house? The answer should be that it doesn't, for the agreed scope. Anything outside the scope gets a separate written change that you approve before the work happens.

Red flags that should end the conversation

Some warning signs are subtle. These aren't.

  • A price over the phone with no site visit. Nobody can size a furnace or heat pump for an Ottawa home without seeing the ducts, the panel and the house.
  • "Today only" pricing. A real price is good next week. Pressure to sign on the spot is there to stop you getting a second quote.
  • No permit, no ESA notification, or "you don't need one." Skipping it saves the contractor a fee and leaves you with uninspected electrical work.
  • A push toward a rental when you asked to buy. Rentals have their place, but read the buyout schedule and ask whether a lien gets registered on your home. Many homeowners find out at closing.
  • Cash only, or a big discount for cash. No invoice means no warranty claim, no WSIB coverage and no paper trail.
  • No model numbers on the quote. You can't compare it, and you may not get what you were shown.
  • A heat pump quote with no mention of backup heat or capacity at -15°C. In Ottawa that's the whole conversation. Anyone who skips it is selling the box, not the system.
  • Running down the rebate program instead of explaining it. The paperwork is a normal part of a heat pump sale here. A company that won't do it is saving itself an hour at your expense.

What HVAC work typically costs in Ottawa

Prices move with the size of the house, the equipment and what the install needs, so treat any number given without a site visit as a guess. Here is how the main jobs line up.

Furnace and AC repairs are quoted after diagnosis. Common parts like ignitors, flame sensors, capacitors and contactors keep a typical repair in the range of a few hundred dollars. Blower motors, inducer motors, control boards and compressors cost more, and that's the point where a good company shows you the replacement price beside the repair. Ask for the diagnostic fee when you book, so there's no surprise on the invoice.

A high-efficiency gas furnace starts at $3,300 installed with us. The final price climbs with the BTU size, the venting run, and whether the ducts or the gas line need work.

A cold-climate heat pump starts at $5,500 installed. The outdoor unit size, the choice between keeping your furnace and adding an electric air handler, the electrical work and the line set length all move the number, and the rebate comes off after the fact.

A Rinnai tankless water heater starts at $3,300 installed, with the venting and gas line sizing driving the difference between one house and the next.

Central air conditioners and ductless systems are a written quote, sized from a heat-gain calculation for the house.

Whatever the number, the words you want on the page are "guaranteed" and "written." Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, and the only honest way to compare two companies is on two written quotes for the same scope. How Ottawa's homes change the answer

Ottawa ranks among the coldest large cities in the country, with a long winter of regular -25°C nights followed by a hot, humid summer. That combination shapes every HVAC decision here more than it does in Toronto or Hamilton.

Sizing has to be done for the cold. A heat pump that looks fine on a spec sheet at 8°C may deliver far less at -25°C. Ask any Ottawa HVAC company for the unit's capacity at -15°C and -25°C, and ask how the heat-loss calculation was done. Cold-climate models rated to -30°C, like the Midea EVOX G3 and Kepler lines, hold most of their output at -15°C. Most Ottawa homes on natural gas still do best as a hybrid, with the heat pump carrying the bulk of the season and the furnace taking over below a balance point worked out using your Hydro Ottawa and Enbridge rates. Homes on baseboards, oil or propane use an electric air handler with backup strips instead.

Older homes inside the Greenbelt have their own problems. Houses in the Glebe, Westboro and Alta Vista were built before central air and often before forced-air heat. Ducts were added piece by piece, returns are small, and the upstairs can be a separate climate. A company that hasn't asked about your ductwork before quoting a central system hasn't thought about your house. Ductless heads or a slim ducted unit are often the better route, and a 100 amp panel may need a load calculation before anything 240V goes in.

The suburbs are reaching replacement age. A great many furnaces and air conditioners in Kanata, Orléans, Barrhaven and Nepean went in during the building boom of the 2000s. They're now at the age where one major repair follows another. If you're there, ask for the replacement price next to every big repair and compare the two with a clear head.

Snow is an installation detail. Outdoor units need a raised stand above typical snow depth and room for defrost water to drain. High-efficiency furnace vents need a termination that won't get buried by a drift or the plow. A quote that doesn't mention either tells you the installer hasn't worked through many Ottawa winters.

Rebates and financing for Ottawa homeowners

The main program in 2026 is the Ontario Home Renovation Savings Program, delivered by Save on Energy and Enbridge Gas with support from the Ontario government. For a cold-climate air-source heat pump it pays up to $7,500, with the amount based on the size of the heat pump and on what heats your home today.

Homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood get the higher tier, which in Ottawa covers plenty of older houses on baseboards and rural properties on oil or propane. Homes heated with Enbridge natural gas get a smaller amount, commonly up to $2,000. Ground-source systems can reach $12,000, and a smart thermostat adds $100. A heat pump on its own does not need a home energy assessment first.

Three rules decide whether you see the money. The heat pump must be on Natural Resources Canada's qualified products list. Pre-approval must be in place before installation, not after. And program terms can change, so confirm eligibility before you book the install. Any HVAC company in Ottawa worth hiring will check the list, submit the pre-approval and file the final documents as part of the job. Our heat pump rebate page keeps the current details in one place.

On financing, the usual options are 0% interest for 3, 6 or 9 months, or an open loan from 2.99% on approved credit, with rates subject to lender approval. The right question to ask is whether the financing is through a lender you can name and read the terms from, or a rental-style agreement in disguise. The financing page lays out how ours works.

The checklist to bring to every quote

Print this or keep it on your phone. If a company can't tick every box, you've learned something useful.

  • TSSA fuels contractor registration and the technician's gas certificate shown on request
  • Licensed refrigeration technician for any heat pump, AC or ductless work
  • ECRA/ESA licence number for the electrical work, checked on the ESA lookup
  • WSIB clearance certificate and liability insurance certificate provided
  • Site visit done before the price was given
  • Room-by-room heat-loss calculation, with the unit's capacity at -15°C stated
  • Written quote with model numbers for every piece of equipment
  • Backup heat plan for -25°C nights and the balance point explained
  • Outdoor unit stand height and vent termination location addressed
  • Guaranteed final price for the agreed scope, with change orders in writing
  • Rebate eligibility confirmed and pre-approval filed before the install date
  • Warranty terms in writing, and who registers it
  • After-hours service explained, with a number you can actually call
  • Financing terms named, with the lender and the rate on paper

Two or three quotes compared on this list will tell you more than any review site. In Ottawa the right system is one you'll live with for fifteen winters, so take your time.
