Problems we fix in older St. Catharines houses and rentals

Older homes and rental properties tend to share the same handful of headaches. Below, each one comes with its cause and our fix.

  • The furnace dates back to the 1990s and the repair bills keep stacking up

    Past 20 years, parts get scarce and efficiency trails a modern 96% AFUE furnace by a wide margin. Our TSSA-certified gas techs tell you plainly whether one more repair makes sense or whether the money is better spent on a replacement.

  • Radiators keep you warm in winter, but summer means window units

    Century homes downtown were built around a boiler. We keep the boiler and add ductless heat pump heads to the bedrooms and main floor, which cool quietly in summer and take over the heating in spring and fall.

  • Tenants phone about no heat on a Friday night

    An unreliable furnace in a rental costs you more than the repair. We take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays, and when a unit is past saving, we quote a replacement that will hold up to years of tenant use.

  • Back bedrooms in your post-war bungalow never warm up

    Bungalows from the 1950s and 1960s often have small ducts and one central return. We measure static pressure, balance the runs and add return air where rooms are starved, then size new equipment to suit.

  • The basement smells damp and the house feels sticky

    Humid summers and damp, lake-effect winters both load the house with moisture. A correctly sized variable-speed AC or heat pump runs long, slow cycles that wring out humidity, and an HRV or ERV keeps fresh air moving.

  • A house full of students cranks the thermostat to 26°C

    A smart thermostat with a locked temperature range keeps the house comfortable without letting anyone turn it into a sauna. It also lets you check on the system from home.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

St. Catharines HVAC facts that shape your system

St. Catharines is mild for Ontario, but the moisture and the age of the housing still make demands on any system.

  • HousingCentury homes to 1980s splits

  • ClimateHumid summers, damp winters

  • RebatesHigher tier for non-gas homes

Heat pump installation in St. Catharines and Thorold: ducts or ductless?

The first question we ask in any older Niagara home is simple: does the house have ducts? The answer decides almost everything that follows.

If it does, a central cold-climate heat pump can replace the old air conditioner and run through the same ducts. Keep the gas furnace as backup and you have a hybrid system, where the heat pump covers most of the heating season and the furnace only fires up when it gets truly cold. We check the ducts and return air first, since heat pumps move more air than a furnace at a gentler temperature.

If it doesn't, as with many radiator homes in the older parts of the city, ductless is usually the answer. Each indoor head needs one small opening in the wall, and one outdoor unit can serve several rooms. The boiler stays, and the radiators get a lot less work.

Thorold has the same mix of older brick homes and newer builds, so the same two routes apply there.

  • Manual J heat-loss sizing for every heat pump quote
  • Midea EVOX G3 or Kepler units rated to -30°C
  • 240V circuit and panel review by ESA-licensed electricians
  • Rebate pre-approval filed before installation

Frequently asked questions

Is Thorold part of your St. Catharines service area?

Yes. Our crews work in Thorold and across St. Catharines, and every quote comes in writing with a guaranteed price.

Can I add a heat pump to a St. Catharines home that has a boiler?

Yes. A ductless heat pump adds cooling and efficient heating without removing the boiler, which stays in place as backup for the coldest days.

What HVAC setup works for a student rental near Brock University?

A high-efficiency furnace or heat pump with a smart thermostat set to a locked temperature range works well. It keeps tenants comfortable and stops the thermostat from being pushed to extremes.

My furnace is past 20. Should I keep fixing it?

Sometimes, for a small part on an otherwise sound unit. If repairs are frequent or the heat exchanger is suspect, a new furnace from $3,300 installed is usually the better spend.

What will furnace installation in St. Catharines set me back?

Expect from $3,300 installed for a high-efficiency furnace, or from $5,500 if you'd rather go with a cold-climate heat pump. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, for example whether a boiler home needs ductless heads, and your signed quote is the final number for that scope.

Who looks after permits for HVAC work in St. Catharines?

We do. As your HVAC contractor in St. Catharines, we pull the permits that apply to your job, and all gas work is done by TSSA-certified technicians.
