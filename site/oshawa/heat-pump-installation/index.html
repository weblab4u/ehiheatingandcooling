Old brick, old ducts, new heat pump. It works when the airflow is measured first.

Heat pump installation in Oshawa is mostly a question about ductwork. The brick homes near downtown and the post-war bungalows around them have supply runs sized for a furnace from another era, and a heat pump needs more air per ton than that furnace ever did. We measure before we size, pair the heat pump with your gas furnace where that makes sense, and handle the rebate paperwork for homes across Durham Region and Ontario.

What gets in the way of a heat pump in an older Oshawa home

None of these are deal breakers. They are the things we look for during the quote visit so they never become surprises on install day.

  • Your ducts were sized for a 1960s furnace

    A heat pump moves roughly 400 cubic feet of air per minute per ton, and old trunk lines were never built for that. We measure static pressure, compare supply and return sizes to the airflow the unit needs, and size the heat pump to what the ducts can carry. Where a return is missing, we add one.

  • The furnace is fine but the air conditioner just died

    That is the ideal moment for a hybrid. The heat pump takes the old AC's spot on the furnace plenum, uses the furnace blower, and handles cooling plus most of the heating. The furnace stays as the backup for the coldest nights, so nothing you already paid for goes to waste.

  • The upstairs of a wartime house has no return air

    Many 1940s and 1950s homes have supply registers upstairs and not a single return, so warm air pools under the roof in summer and the heat pump cannot do its job. We add a return where the framing allows, or put a ductless head in the upstairs bedroom and let the ducted system serve the main floor.

  • You heat with gas and wonder if the rebate is worth the bother

    Enbridge-heated homes get the smaller Home Renovation Savings Program amount, commonly up to $2,000, and the paperwork is ours to handle, not yours. We build the running-cost comparison from your real Oshawa Power and Enbridge rates so you can see what the heat pump saves before you sign.

  • An addition or attic conversion runs on electric baseboards

    Baseboards turn one unit of electricity into one unit of heat. A ductless cold-climate heat pump for that space gives several units of heat for each unit of hydro, and a home heated mainly by electricity can qualify for the higher rebate tier, up to $7,500.

  • The water heater still vents up the old chimney

    A heat pump on its own does not touch the chimney. If you replace the furnace at the same time, though, the chimney is left serving only the water heater, and that raises the liner question. We plan the furnace, water heater and chimney together so you get one answer, not three.

  • Lake wind through a drafty brick wall makes you doubt a heat pump

    The heat-loss number decides this, not the age of the house. We calculate it room by room, then check the chosen unit's capacity at -15°C against it. The Midea EVOX G3 and Kepler models we install are rated to -30°C, and in a hybrid the furnace covers whatever is left.

  • The furnace closet has no room for a bigger coil

    Older bungalows often have the furnace tucked in a closet with inches to spare. We measure the plenum height, the clearances and the condensate route before we pick the coil and air handler, so the quote matches the space you have.

Hybrid or all-electric? The Oshawa Power and Enbridge math

Most Oshawa homes come to us with a working gas furnace and a dead or dying air conditioner, so the practical question is whether to keep the furnace. In almost every case the answer is yes, at least for now. A hybrid system lets the heat pump carry cooling and the heating from roughly October to April on most days, while the furnace fires only on the deep-cold nights when gas is the cheaper fuel.

Where the furnace changes is the balance point, the outdoor temperature at which the thermostat hands over from heat pump to furnace. We set it from your actual Oshawa Power electricity rate and your Enbridge gas rate, not a rule of thumb. The colder it gets, the less heat the heat pump makes per dollar, and the balance point is where the two fuels cost the same.

  • Gas furnace in good shape: hybrid, with the furnace as backup
  • Furnace near the end of its life: hybrid with a new furnace, or an electric air handler if you want off gas
  • Baseboard-heated addition: ductless heat pump for that space, higher rebate tier may apply
  • Ducts too small to fix: ductless multi-zone instead of forcing a ducted unit

Changing the furnace at the same time raises the venting and chimney questions we cover on our Oshawa HVAC page. For ratings, HSPF2 and backup options for homes anywhere in Ontario, read the province-wide cold-climate heat pump page.

Frequently asked questions

How much does heat pump installation cost in Oshawa?

Cold-climate heat pump installation in Oshawa starts at $5,500 installed. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, such as duct repairs, an added return or panel work in an older home, and every item is listed on a guaranteed written quote.

Will a heat pump work with the old ductwork in my Oshawa house?

Usually, once the ducts are measured. We test static pressure and compare duct sizes to the airflow the heat pump needs, then size the unit to match or add a return where a room is starved. If the ducts truly cannot carry it, a ductless system is the honest alternative.

Should I keep my gas furnace when I add a heat pump?

Yes, if the furnace is in good condition. A hybrid system uses the heat pump for cooling and most heating days and the furnace for the coldest nights, and we set the switch-over point from your Oshawa Power and Enbridge rates.

How long does a heat pump installation take in an older Oshawa home?

A hybrid installation on existing ducts is usually a one-day job. Adding a return, replacing the furnace at the same time or upgrading the panel can stretch it, and the quote states the schedule so you are not guessing.

Does an Oshawa home on Enbridge gas qualify for the heat pump rebate?

It can. Gas-heated homes get the smaller Home Renovation Savings Program tier, commonly up to $2,000, while homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood can receive up to $7,500. The model must be on NRCan's qualified list and pre-approval must be in place before installation, and program terms can change.

Can I finance a heat pump in Oshawa and still get the rebate?

Yes. Financing is available at 0% interest for 3, 6 or 9 months or as an open loan from 2.99% on approved credit, and the rebate is paid after the installation is complete.
