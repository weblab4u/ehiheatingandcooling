AC repair in Chatham-Kent: what a long cooling season wears out

Months of hard work in heat and humidity take a toll. Here are the problems we're called about, and the fixes.

  • The basement smells musty by mid-July

    Cool basements collect moisture when the AC never runs long enough to dry the air. We look at sizing, blower speed and drainage, and add dehumidification where the house needs it.

  • The outdoor unit gives out near the end of a long summer

    Months of heavy use wear down capacitors, contactors and fan motors. Our licensed refrigeration techs test each part and replace the weak ones before they quit on a hot day.

  • Dust from nearby fields clogs the outdoor coil

    Rural air carries dust and chaff that blankets the coil. A clogged coil can't shed heat, so the unit runs hotter and costs more. We clean it and suggest a better spot if it sits downwind of a field.

  • A puddle shows up under the furnace every summer

    Long cooling seasons push a lot of condensate through the drain, and a clogged trap or tired pump overflows. We clear the line, correct the slope and fit a float switch that stops the AC before water spills.

  • The old furnace in a Chatham house is loud and uneven

    Older single-stage furnaces blast on and off. A two-stage, variable-speed furnace runs at low output most of the time, which is quieter and steadier, and it pairs well with a heat pump.

  • You heat a farmhouse with propane and the bills sting

    In this milder climate a cold-climate heat pump can carry most of your heating, with the propane furnace kept as backup. Propane-heated homes may qualify for the higher rebate tier.

  • The thermostat reads 22°C but the house still feels like a greenhouse

    Temperature is only half the picture between the lakes. A thermostat that also reads humidity, paired with a variable-speed system that can run a dehumidify mode, lets the AC keep working on moisture after the temperature target is met. Most people in Chatham and Tilbury find 22°C at lower humidity feels better than 20°C in sticky air, and it costs less to hold.

Why a slightly smaller AC dries a Chatham-Kent house better

It sounds backwards, but an air conditioner that's a little smaller than the old one often leaves the house more comfortable in a humid July.

Cooling and drying happen on the same indoor coil. The coil only pulls water out of the air once it's cold and the air has been moving across it for a while. A big unit hits the thermostat setting in ten minutes and shuts off before the coil has done much drying, then starts again twenty minutes later. A correctly sized variable-speed unit runs for an hour at low speed, and that long, gentle cycle is what wrings the moisture out.

So we size from a heat-gain calculation that counts your windows, insulation and the direction the house faces, rather than matching the tonnage on the old label. Between Lake Erie and Lake St. Clair, that calculation is the difference between a cool house and a cool, clammy one.

Frequently asked questions

Can a heat pump handle humidity in a Chatham-Kent summer?

Yes, if it's sized correctly. An inverter heat pump runs longer at low speed, which pulls more moisture from the air than an oversized unit that shuts off quickly.

Is a heat pump enough for winter in Chatham-Kent?

For most of the winter, yes. Winters here are milder than in much of Ontario and cold-climate models are rated to heat down to -30°C, though many homes keep a furnace as backup.

Will my propane-heated farmhouse earn a heat pump rebate?

Many do. Homes heated by propane, oil, wood or electricity may qualify for up to $7,500 based on heat pump size, with pre-approval needed before installation.

Is AC repair in Chatham-Kent possible on a summer weekend?

Yes. We take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays, and you get a written price before any repair.

Which towns get HVAC services in Chatham-Kent from your crews?

We serve Chatham, Wallaceburg, Blenheim, Tilbury, Ridgetown, Dresden, Erieau and the rural areas between them, working from our office in Stouffville.

How often should my AC be serviced with such a long cooling season?

Once a year, in spring before the heat arrives. We clean the coil, test the electrical parts, check the refrigerant charge and clear the condensate drain.

How much does AC installation cost in Chatham-Kent?

A central air conditioner in Chatham-Kent is priced by written quote after a heat-gain calculation sets the size. A cold-climate heat pump, which cools the same way and also heats, starts from $5,500 installed, and a high-efficiency furnace from $3,300. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements.
