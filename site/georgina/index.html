What an HVAC technician in Georgina checks in a converted cottage

Places built for summer weekends need a closer look before they get a year-round system.

  • Insulation in the walls, floor and attic, since many cottages started with very little
  • Crawl spaces and floors over open air that pull heat out from below
  • The electrical panel's size and space for a 240V heat pump circuit
  • How the home heats today: baseboards, a wood stove, propane or oil
  • Wall space for ductless heads and a clean route for the line set
  • A spot for the outdoor unit out of the north wind and above drifting snow
  • Whether there's room for ducts at all, or ductless is the better route
  • Your rebate tier, based on the fuel you heat with now

Heating and cooling in Keswick and Sutton when the lake wind picks up

Lakeshore homes in Georgina take the full force of the wind coming across Lake Simcoe, and the snow piles up fast. A house that felt fine in July can lose heat quickly through old windows, thin walls and a floor over a crawl space. That's the challenge with a cottage that's now lived in all year.

For homes heated with baseboards, a ductless heat pump is usually the simplest upgrade. Slim wall heads go in the main rooms, one outdoor unit sits on a stand or bracket, and the refrigerant lines pass through a small hole in the wall. You get efficient heat in winter, cooling on muggy summer nights, and the baseboards can stay as backup.

For homes on natural gas or propane with ductwork, we size the furnace from a proper heat-loss calculation that accounts for the wind exposure. Bigger isn't better. An oversized furnace short cycles and leaves cold spots, while one matched to the house runs longer, steadier cycles.

  • Cold-climate units rated to heat down to -30°C
  • Outdoor units raised above drifts and turned away from the lake wind
  • Baseboards kept as backup if you want them
  • Homes heated by electricity, oil or propane may qualify for up to $7,500

Frequently asked questions

Can a heat pump heat a converted cottage in Georgina through winter?

Yes, if the home is reasonably insulated and the heat pump is sized from a heat-loss calculation. Cold-climate units are rated to -30°C, and many owners keep baseboards as backup.

What's the best heating for a Keswick home with baseboards?

A ductless heat pump is usually the best fit, since it needs no ducts and costs less to run than baseboard heat. One outdoor unit can serve several rooms.

Do I need to insulate before installing a heat pump?

Not always, but it helps. Better insulation lets a smaller, less costly heat pump do the job, and we'll point out where upgrades would make the biggest difference.

What rebate can a baseboard-heated home in Georgina get?

Homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood can qualify for up to $7,500 through the Home Renovation Savings Program, based on heat pump size. Pre-approval has to be granted before the installation date.

Do you offer furnace repair in Georgina for propane furnaces?

Yes. Our TSSA-certified gas technicians repair natural gas and propane furnaces across Georgina, with a written price before work begins.

Can I book heat pump installation in Georgina outside Keswick, in Sutton or Pefferlaw?

Yes. We cover Keswick, Sutton, Jackson's Point, Pefferlaw and the rest of Georgina.
