Wasaga Beach homes and heating, at a glance

Homes along the bay were often built for summer first and winter second. That shapes what heating system makes sense.

  • Typical homesConverted cottages and bungalows

    Former cottages now lived in year-round, single-storey bungalows, and newer subdivisions further from the water.

  • Heating sourceMany on electric baseboards

    With baseboards, every kilowatt-hour you pay for becomes the same amount of heat and no more. A heat pump moves heat instead of making it, so it does more with the same power.

  • ClimateGeorgian Bay wind, Snowbelt snow

    Lake-effect snow and wind off the bay mean the outdoor unit needs a raised stand and the system needs real cold-weather capacity.

  • RebateUp to $7,500

    Electrically heated homes get the higher tier, based on heat pump size. Homes on Enbridge gas get a smaller amount. Pre-approval comes first.

Baseboard and cottage problems an HVAC technician in Wasaga Beach solves

These are the comfort and cost problems that come up again and again, from the older places along Mosley Street and River Road to the newer streets out by Wasaga Sands.

  • The hydro bill climbs every month the baseboards run

    Baseboards are simple but costly to run. In mild weather a cold-climate heat pump can move two to three units of heat for each unit of electricity it uses, so the same warmth costs a lot less.

  • The cottage was never built for a real winter

    Thin walls and crawlspaces lose heat fast. Our heat-loss calculation shows where the heat goes, we size each indoor head to its room, and we point out the drafts worth sealing first.

  • Window AC units rattle all summer and cool one room

    Ductless heads are quiet, mount high on the wall and cool the whole space evenly. You get your windows and your sleep back.

  • There's no basement and no room for ductwork

    Ductless needs only a small hole through the wall for the line set and a drain. Slab-on-grade bungalows and crawlspace cottages are a good fit.

  • You're away for weeks and worry about the place in January

    We set the heat pump to hold a steady low temperature and leave your baseboards in place as backup. Ask us about thermostat and control options before you head south.

  • Wind off the bay makes the north rooms the coldest in the house

    We place indoor heads where they can reach the cold walls and pick equipment rated to -30°C, with enough output for those rooms on a windy night.

  • The Shore Lane place is a weekend spot today and might be your retirement home later

    Then we size it for full-time living now. A multi-zone outdoor unit can start with two heads and take more later, so the back bedroom or a future sunroom gets added without a second outdoor unit. Planning it this way costs little extra up front and saves tearing out line sets in five years.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Frequently asked questions

Can a ductless heat pump replace baseboard heaters in Wasaga Beach?

For most of the year, yes. A properly sized ductless system can handle the bulk of your heating, and we usually leave the baseboards in place as backup for the coldest nights.

How much rebate can a baseboard-heated home get?

Homes heated by electricity can get up to $7,500 through the Home Renovation Savings Program, based on heat pump size. The heat pump must be on NRCan's qualified list, and pre-approval must be in place before installation.

Is a converted cottage too drafty for a heat pump?

Usually not. We run a heat-loss calculation to size the system for the house as it is, and we point out the few air-sealing fixes that make the biggest difference.

Can a heat pump keep my place warm while I'm away for the winter?

Yes. It can hold a steady low temperature, and keeping the baseboards as backup adds a safety net if the power or the unit has a problem.

Do you provide HVAC services in Wasaga Beach year-round?

Yes. Our office is in Stouffville, and we book installs, repairs and service visits in Wasaga Beach in every season, from Beach Area 1 to the Beach Area 6 end and out to Wasaga Sands.

What does it cost to put ductless heat into a Wasaga Beach cottage?

A single-zone ductless system is priced in a written quote after we see the room, because the head size, the line-set run and the electrical work change the number. Ducted cold-climate heat pumps start from $5,500 installed, and every quote is guaranteed before work starts.

How many indoor heads does a Wasaga Beach bungalow need?

It depends on the layout, how open the rooms are and how you use them. We set the number of heads from a room-by-room heat-loss check, and one outdoor unit can run several of them.
