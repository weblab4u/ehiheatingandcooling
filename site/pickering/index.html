Pickering homes at a glance, and what they need

Pickering has some of the newest homes in Durham Region and some of the oldest along the lake. Both can end up with a heat pump, for different reasons.

  • Housing mixSeaton new builds to 1960s Bay Ridges

    Newer homes in Duffin Heights and Seaton often have good ducts and builder-basic equipment. Older Bay Ridges homes tend to have aging AC and long-serving furnaces.

  • UtilitiesElexicon Energy and Enbridge Gas

    Before we suggest going electric, we look at your Elexicon rates and your Enbridge gas use, so the savings estimate fits your house.

  • WeatherLake humidity, cold winter wind

    Summer air off Lake Ontario is heavy with moisture, and winter wind off the water pulls heat out of the house. Equipment has to handle both.

  • Rebate tierUp to $7,500

    Electric, oil or propane heat qualifies for the higher amount. Gas-heated homes usually get a smaller one, commonly up to $2,000.

Calls a Pickering HVAC technician gets every season

New house or old, these are the issues that bring Pickering homeowners to us. Each one has a practical fix.

  • Your Bay Ridges air conditioner is older than your kids

    Many of these units still run on R-22, a refrigerant that is no longer made, so even a small leak gets expensive. We replace the old condenser with a cold-climate heat pump that cools all summer and heats most of the winter, reusing your ducts when they test sound.

  • Your new Seaton home came with a basic furnace and no AC

    Builder packages often skip cooling entirely. We add a ducted heat pump to the existing furnace and ductwork, which gives you air conditioning now and a lower gas bill in the shoulder seasons.

  • You want to move off gas, just not all at once

    We plan it in stages. Start with a hybrid system that keeps the furnace as backup, then switch to an electric air handler paired with a Midea EVOX G3 heat pump when the furnace reaches the end of its life.

  • Your electrical panel has no space for a heat pump circuit

    A ducted heat pump needs its own 240V circuit. Our ESA-licensed electrical work covers the new breaker, the disconnect and a panel upgrade if your load calls for one, all on the same quote.

  • Summer air indoors feels damp and sticky

    An oversized single-stage AC cools the air fast and shuts off before it pulls the moisture out. An inverter heat pump runs longer at low speed, which dries the air and keeps lakeshore humidity in check.

  • You doubt a heat pump can handle February wind off the lake

    Fair question. Cold-climate models are rated down to -30°C and keep strong capacity at -15°C. We size from a heat-loss calculation and plan backup heat for the coldest nights, so you never rely on a guess.

Frequently asked questions

Should I replace my old AC in Pickering with a heat pump?

In most cases, yes, if the AC is near the end of its life. A cold-climate heat pump costs a bit more than a plain AC but also heats your home for much of the winter, and it may qualify for a rebate.

Can a heat pump be the only heat in a new Seaton home?

Often it can, when the house is well built and the heat pump is sized from a proper heat-loss calculation. We pair it with an electric air handler that has backup heat strips for the coldest nights.

Do I need a panel upgrade for a heat pump in Pickering?

Not always. Many homes have room for the 240V circuit a heat pump needs, and we check your panel and load before quoting so any upgrade is priced up front.

How does the rebate work for a gas-heated Pickering home?

Homes heated with Enbridge gas usually qualify for a smaller rebate, commonly up to $2,000, under the Home Renovation Savings Program. Program terms can change, and pre-approval has to be done before the install, so we confirm eligibility first.

Can I keep my furnace and still add a heat pump?

Yes. That setup is called a hybrid system, and it lets the heat pump handle most of the year while the furnace takes over in deep cold.

Is a Pickering HVAC technician available on Sundays?

Yes. We are open Sunday 10 am to 5 pm, Monday to Saturday 8 am to 8 pm, and we take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays.
