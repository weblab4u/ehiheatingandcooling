What an HVAC contractor in Kingston checks in a heritage home

Old stone and brick houses hide surprises. These are the things we look at before we suggest any equipment.

  • Wall thickness and material, so we know how to core a clean line set opening
  • Where an outdoor unit can sit without crowding a heritage facade or a neighbour's window
  • Attic, closet and bulkhead space that could hide a slim-duct air handler
  • Room-by-room heat loss, since stone walls behave very differently from insulated frame walls
  • Electrical panel capacity for a 240V heat pump circuit
  • The current heat source, which affects your rebate tier
  • Existing radiators or ducts we can keep using as backup
  • Condensate drain routes that won't stain stone or freeze in winter

Heat pump installation in Kingston when ducts won't fit

Running new ducts through a limestone house usually means opening plaster, losing closet space and boxing in ceilings. Most owners don't want that, and in many downtown homes it just isn't practical.

There are two cleaner routes. A ductless heat pump mounts a slim head high on an inside wall, so each room or zone gets its own control and the walls stay intact. A slim-duct unit hides in an attic, closet or bulkhead and feeds two or three rooms through short ducts, with only small grilles showing.

A common plan for a Sydenham Ward or Barriefield house mixes the two: slim-duct for the bedrooms upstairs, a ductless head for the main living area, and the existing boiler or furnace kept as backup for deep cold. Kingston winters run colder than the lakeshore GTA, so we size from a proper heat-loss calculation and use cold-climate equipment.

On rebates: if the home is on gas, whether from Utilities Kingston in the core or Enbridge outside it, eligibility depends on the program terms and your utility, and we confirm it before installation. Homes heated by electricity, oil or propane may reach the higher tier of up to $7,500.

Frequently asked questions

Can you install a heat pump in a limestone house in Kingston?

Yes. Ductless and slim-duct heat pumps need only a small opening through the wall for the refrigerant lines, so they suit stone homes where new ductwork isn't practical.

What is a slim-duct heat pump?

A slim-duct heat pump is a compact indoor unit that hides in an attic, closet or bulkhead and feeds a few rooms through short ducts. You see small grilles instead of wall-mounted heads.

Do Utilities Kingston gas customers qualify for the heat pump rebate?

It depends. For gas-heated homes, eligibility depends on the program terms and your utility, and we confirm it before installation. Homes heated by electricity, oil or propane may qualify for up to $7,500.

Do you service student rentals near Queen's?

Yes. We repair and upgrade heating and cooling in rental houses near the university and we book entry times with tenants once you, the owner, sign off.

Will a ductless unit look out of place in a heritage home?

It doesn't have to. We place heads high on interior walls, run lines outside behind paintable covers, and choose spots for the outdoor unit that stay out of view from the street where possible.

Is furnace repair in Kingston available on weekends?

Yes. We take emergency furnace calls evenings, weekends and holidays. Call 1-647-694-5845 and we'll tell you when a TSSA-certified technician can get to you.
