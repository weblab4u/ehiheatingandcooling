Air conditioner problems Hamilton homes call about

What you notice, what it usually means in this part of Ontario, and what we do. For everything else we handle in the city, see our Hamilton HVAC page.

  • The house in Kirkendall feels sticky even though the AC runs

    An oversized single-stage unit cools the air fast and shuts off before it has pulled the moisture out. Lower-city humidity makes this worse. We check sizing, airflow and charge; sometimes the fix is a fan speed change, sometimes it's an honest talk about a smaller inverter unit.

  • Ice on the copper line beside the furnace

    Older Mountain bungalows often have ducts too small for the AC coil that was added later. Starved airflow freezes the coil. We thaw it, measure static pressure and temperature drop, and fix the cause, which may be a filter, a blower or a refrigerant leak.

  • The ductless head in your radiator home drips down the wall

    A blocked condensate line or a head that has lost its slight tilt sends water onto the plaster. We clear the drain, re-level the head and clean the filters and blower wheel. Ductless units in a dusty city need that cleaning every couple of years.

  • The outdoor unit between two brick walls trips on hot days

    A condenser squeezed into a narrow side yard breathes its own hot air and overheats. We clean the coil, check the fan motor and clearances, and if the spot is hopeless we'll suggest a wall bracket or a better location.

  • Condenser packed with cottonwood seed and grass clippings

    Late June fluff coats the fins and the unit can't shed heat. A proper coil cleaning with the right chemical restores capacity and drops the amp draw. We test the capacitor while the panel is off.

  • Builder AC in Stoney Creek or Waterdown hums but the fan won't spin

    The run capacitor is the most common single failure on 10-to-15-year-old units, and it's a quick swap. We also test the contactor and compressor amps so you're not back on the phone in August.

  • Your older R-22 unit needs a top-up every summer

    R-22 is no longer produced, so each top-up costs more and the leak is still there. We find it with nitrogen and an electronic detector. On a 15-year-old unit we'll usually show you the replacement price beside the repair.

  • Water on the basement floor beside the furnace in July

    That's the condensate drain from the AC coil, blocked with slime or missing a trap. We clear the line, add a trap if there isn't one and check the drain pan for cracks. In a low lower-city basement we often add a condensate pump.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

The modern replacement for a worn-out AC in Hamilton

A cold-climate heat pump is a central air conditioner that also runs in reverse to heat. If your AC has failed for good, one of these units replaces it on the same ducts, cools exactly like an AC, and takes over most of your heating from the furnace or boiler. When we'll tell a Hamilton homeowner to stop repairing the AC

We fix a lot of air conditioners that other companies would have condemned. A capacitor, a contactor, a fan motor or a drain on a ten-year-old unit is routine, and we'd rather do it than sell you a system you don't need.

The line gets crossed in three places. First, a compressor failure on a unit past 12 years. Second, a leak inside the evaporator coil on an R-22 system, where the refrigerant alone makes the repair poor value. Third, a unit that was oversized from day one and has never kept a lower-city home dry, because no repair fixes a sizing mistake.

At that point we show two prices: a like-for-like central AC, and a cold-climate heat pump on the same ducts. For a radiator home with ductless heads, it's a new multi-zone system. The heat pump usually wins because it cools identically, cuts gas use in spring and fall, and can bring back a rebate through the Home Renovation Savings Program if the model is on NRCan's qualified list and pre-approval is done before installation.

  • Small electrical part on a unit under 12 years: repair
  • Compressor or coil on an older unit: compare both prices
  • R-22 leak: usually replace
  • Oversized and clammy: resize, don't patch

Frequently asked questions

How much does AC repair cost in Hamilton?

AC repair in Hamilton is quoted in writing after diagnosis, because the price depends on the failed part. A capacitor or contactor is a modest repair; a compressor or evaporator coil is not. If replacement is the better value, a cold-climate heat pump that cools like a central AC starts at $5,500 installed.

Why does my lower-city house feel damp even with the AC on?

An air conditioner that is too big for the house cools the air quickly and shuts off before it has removed much moisture, which is why oversized units in humid lower-city neighbourhoods leave rooms feeling clammy. Longer run times, a lower fan speed or a correctly sized inverter unit fix it.

Do you repair ductless mini splits in Hamilton?

Yes. We service ductless heads and outdoor units in Hamilton's radiator homes and additions: dripping heads, units blowing warm, error codes and dirty blower wheels. Most are fixed on the first visit.

How fast can you get to an AC repair in Hamilton?

We book AC repair in Hamilton seven days a week, with emergency calls in the evenings and on holidays. Call 1-647-694-5845 and we'll give you an honest booking time rather than a promise we can't keep.

Should I replace my old AC with a heat pump?

If your air conditioner is past 12 years and needs a major repair, a cold-climate heat pump is usually the better buy. It cools the same way, heats for most of the year, and a qualifying model can return a rebate through the Home Renovation Savings Program. We quote both so you can compare.

Can I finance an AC or heat pump replacement?

Yes. Financing runs 0% interest for 3, 6 or 9 months, or open loans from 2.99% on approved credit, with rates subject to lender approval. The written quote shows the monthly option beside the cash price.
