Lake Erie living and what it means for your HVAC

Lakeside homes deal with weather that inland houses never see. These local facts shape every Fort Erie system we plan.

  • HomesCottages to new subdivisions

    Older houses in town, lakeside homes and cottages in Crystal Beach and Ridgeway, and newer subdivisions each call for a different setup.

  • UtilitiesCanadian Niagara Power and Enbridge Gas

    We compare your Canadian Niagara Power rates with your gas, propane or oil costs before suggesting how much a heat pump should carry.

  • ClimateLake winds and lake-effect snow

    Wind off the water raises heat loss on the exposed side of the house, and squalls can bury an outdoor unit placed too low.

  • RebatesHigher tier off the gas line

    Cottages and homes on baseboards, oil, propane or wood can earn up to $7,500 toward a qualifying heat pump. Pre-approval comes first.

What an HVAC contractor in Fort Erie fixes along the lake

Shoreline homes and cottages share a short list of headaches. Every one of them can be solved.

  • Your cottage is too cold to enjoy once Thanksgiving passes

    Baseboards and a wood stove only go so far. A ductless heat pump heats efficiently through fall and spring and cools in summer, so the place stays usable for far more of the year.

  • The outdoor unit near the water looks worn after a few seasons

    Wet air, wind-blown sand and road salt are hard on coils and cabinets. We set the unit on the sheltered side of the house, raise it off damp ground, keep it clear of spray and drip lines, and show you how to rinse the coil each spring.

  • Wind off the lake cuts straight through the house in January

    Exposed walls lose heat much faster on a windy night. We size from a room-by-room Manual J heat-loss calculation, choose equipment rated to -30°C and plan backup heat so the house stays warm when it blows hardest.

  • Electric baseboards are draining your budget

    On a mild day, a cold-climate heat pump can deliver two to three units of heat for every unit of electricity. Baseboard-heated homes also tend to land in the top rebate tier.

  • A lake-effect squall buries the outdoor unit

    We mount it on a stand or wall bracket above where snow tends to drift, out from under the eaves, with a clear path for defrost water to drain away.

  • An older furnace in town keeps quitting on cold mornings

    Our TSSA-certified gas technicians check the ignitor, flame sensor, pressure switch and venting. If the furnace is near the end, you get a written repair price next to a replacement or hybrid option.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Frequently asked questions

Is a ductless heat pump enough to heat a Fort Erie cottage through January?

Yes, if it's a cold-climate model sized for the building. Units rated to -30°C keep producing heat in deep cold, and for year-round homes our heat pump installation in Fort Erie includes a backup heat plan.

Where should the outdoor unit go on a lakefront property?

On the side of the house sheltered from the lake, raised off damp ground and clear of roof drip lines and spray. We decide the exact spot after walking the property.

Can a heat pump keep an empty cottage above freezing?

A heat pump can hold a low setpoint while you're away, but for a place left empty in deep winter we recommend backup heat and a smart thermostat that alerts you if the temperature drops.

What heat pump rebate can a Fort Erie home on electric baseboards get?

If baseboards, oil, propane or wood heat the house, the rebate can reach $7,500, scaled to heat pump size. The approval has to land before installation day, and program terms may change, so we check first.

Do you take no-heat calls in Fort Erie on weekends?

Yes, emergency HVAC in Fort Erie covers weekends, as well as evenings and holidays. Ring 1-647-694-5845, and we'll give you a realistic arrival window based on the roads and the weather.
