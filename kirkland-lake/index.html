Insulation-aware sizing for heating and cooling in Kirkland Lake

An older home that's been upgraded loses heat very differently from the day it was built. These are the things we check before we size anything.

  • Attic insulation depth and how well the attic hatch seals
  • Wall construction, from original plaster to newer insulated framing
  • Window age and drafts around the frames
  • Basement walls and rim joists, a big heat-loss spot in older homes
  • Additions built at different times with different insulation
  • The size and condition of the existing ductwork
  • Panel capacity for a 240V heat pump circuit
  • Your current fuel and what can serve as backup heat

Why a hybrid makes sense for nights well below -30°C

The cold-climate heat pumps we install are rated to heat down to -30°C. In Kirkland Lake, that covers most of the winter, but not the nights that go well past it. A hybrid system handles those nights. The heat pump runs through fall, spring and the milder part of winter, and a furnace or electric heat takes over when it gets truly bitter.

Insulation changes the math. If you've added attic insulation, new windows or spray foam in the basement since the old furnace went in, the house now needs less heat. An oversized furnace short cycles, wears out faster and leaves cold spots between runs. We recalculate the heat loss for the house as it is today, then size the heat pump and the backup to match.

Homes on gas usually keep the furnace as backup. Homes on oil, propane, wood or electricity can choose an electric air handler or keep their existing furnace, and those homes can qualify for the higher rebate tier of up to $7,500. We confirm eligibility and file pre-approval ahead of the install, since program terms can change.

Frequently asked questions

Can a heat pump work in Kirkland Lake when it drops past -30°C?

A heat pump's output falls below its rated range, so in Kirkland Lake it should always have backup heat. In a hybrid system, the furnace or electric heat takes over automatically on those nights.

I added insulation. Is my furnace now too big?

It might be. A furnace sized for the house before the upgrade can short cycle afterward, and a new heat-loss calculation will tell us for sure.

What is insulation-aware sizing?

Insulation-aware sizing means calculating your heating needs from the walls, attic, windows and basement as they are today, not from the age of the house or the old furnace label. It keeps new equipment from being oversized.

Is it worth fixing an older furnace in a Kirkland Lake home?

If the heat exchanger is sound and the repair is minor, usually yes. If repairs keep stacking up on an older unit, we'll show you the replacement price next to the repair so you can compare.

Do you take emergency heating calls in Kirkland Lake?

Yes. We take emergency calls evenings, weekends and holidays at 1-647-694-5845, and we'll talk through the problem and your options with you.

Do you install heat pumps in Larder Lake and Englehart?

Yes. We install hybrid and cold-climate heat pump systems in Larder Lake, Englehart and across the area, and we schedule the work with you.
