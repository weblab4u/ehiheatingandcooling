What an HVAC technician in Bradford fixes in newer homes

Newer homes in town have a lot going for them. Even airflow isn't always one of them.

  • The bonus room over the garage is the coldest room in the house

    A room over an unheated garage loses heat through the floor as well as the walls and ceiling, and it's usually at the end of a long duct run. We measure the airflow it actually gets, seal and balance the ducts, and if that's not enough, add a ductless head just for that room.

  • The basement playroom stays chilly all winter

    Basements in newer homes often have only a few supply registers and no dedicated return. We add supply or return air where the layout allows, or a ductless unit, so the kids' space is usable in January.

  • The builder furnace is loud and swings from hot to cold

    Most builder models have a single-stage burner and a basic blower, so they cycle hard. Moving to a two-stage, variable-speed Midea MGH96 or Lennox furnace means long, gentle run times and far less temperature swing.

  • The AC that came with the house can't handle a heat wave

    Builders often install the smallest AC that passes. We run a heat-gain calculation and, when it's time to replace, match a properly sized AC or a cold-climate heat pump to your furnace.

  • The thermostat schedule never matches your family's day

    A basic programmable thermostat set once at move-in rarely fits life with kids. We install and set up a smart thermostat with schedules and phone control, and many qualify for a $100 rebate.

  • Wind across the open fields makes the house drafty

    Bradford sits in open, windy farm country between Lake Simcoe and the GTA. Wind finds gaps at rim joists and around windows, so we check where the house is losing heat before sizing new equipment.

  • The nursery is the warmest room in July and the coldest in January

    Corner bedrooms have two outside walls and often sit at the far end of the duct run, so they swing with the weather more than any other room. We balance the registers, add a damper where the trunk line allows, and set up a smart thermostat sensor in that room so the system responds to it rather than to the hallway. If that still falls short, a small ductless head gives the room its own control.

Builder-grade versus upgraded: what changes in a newer Bradford home

Builder-grade doesn't mean broken. It means the equipment was chosen to meet code at the lowest cost. That usually gets you a single-stage furnace, a basic AC sized close to the minimum, a thermostat in the hallway and ducts that were never balanced.

The house works, but it isn't as comfortable as it could be. Some rooms run warm, some run cold, and the furnace cycles on and off all evening.

An upgrade doesn't always mean new equipment. Sometimes we balance dampers, add a return in the basement and move the thermostat. When the furnace or AC is the real problem, we size a replacement from a room-by-room heat-loss calculation and pick a two-stage or variable-speed model that runs longer, quieter and more evenly.

  • Airflow measured room by room, not guessed
  • Duct balancing and sealing before any new equipment
  • Two-stage, variable-speed furnaces rated 96% AFUE or higher
  • AC or cold-climate heat pump matched to the furnace
  • Smart thermostat placed where it reads the house properly

Frequently asked questions

Why is the room over my garage in Bradford so cold?

It loses heat through the floor, walls and ceiling and usually sits at the end of a long duct run. Duct balancing, sealing or a small ductless unit usually fixes it.

How does a Bradford HVAC contractor fix a cold basement in a newer home?

We start with airflow, since many new basements have too few supply registers and no return. Adding supply or return air, or a ductless head, usually solves it.

Is it worth upgrading a builder-grade furnace?

It's worth it when the furnace is loud, cycles hard, or rooms stay uneven after the ducts are balanced. A two-stage, variable-speed furnace runs longer and quieter, and we'll tell you if the existing one is fine.

Is there a rebate for a smart thermostat in Bradford?

Yes. Eligible smart thermostats can qualify for a $100 rebate through the Home Renovation Savings Program. Confirm eligibility first, as program terms can change.

How much should I set aside for furnace installation in Bradford?

A high-efficiency furnace starts from $3,300 installed. Installed pricing depends on size and installation requirements, and the written quote we give you is guaranteed.

Do you serve Bond Head and the Holland Marsh area?

Yes. We cover Bradford, Bond Head, the Holland Marsh, Newton Robinson and the rest of Bradford West Gwillimbury.

Can a smart thermostat fix uneven rooms on its own?

Not on its own. A smart thermostat with a remote sensor can average the hallway and a bedroom, which helps, but it cannot push more air into a room than the duct can carry. We fix the airflow first, then set up the thermostat to manage it.
