Older home, older system: what an Oshawa HVAC contractor fixes

Homes near downtown and the post-war streets around them share a handful of HVAC headaches. Here is what causes each one and how we sort it out.

  • You were told the chimney needs a liner and nobody said why

    When a new high-efficiency furnace stops using the chimney, the gas water heater can be left alone in a flue that is now too big for it. That can cause poor draft and condensation inside the brick. We price both fixes: a properly sized liner, or a water heater that vents out the wall so the chimney is retired.

  • The furnace is decades old and the gas bill keeps creeping up

    Older mid-efficiency furnaces send a good chunk of their heat up the chimney. A two-stage, variable-speed furnace at 96% to 97% AFUE vents through PVC pipe out the side wall and needs a condensate drain, both of which we plan before install day.

  • Rooms at the end of the duct run never get warm

    Old ducts are often undersized, crushed or leaking at the joints. We test static pressure, seal what we can reach and add returns where rooms are starved, so the new furnace isn't fighting the ductwork.

  • Dust puffs out of the registers when the blower starts

    That usually points to open returns pulling air from the basement and a missing or poorly fitted filter. We seal the return side and install a proper filter cabinet as part of the job.

  • You want a heat pump but can't face replacing the ducts

    A heat pump needs more airflow per ton than most old furnaces were designed for. We measure your ducts first, then size the heat pump to what they can carry, often as a hybrid with your furnace as backup.

  • The furnace room in the bungalow is barely bigger than the furnace

    A properly sized high-efficiency furnace is often smaller in BTU than the one it replaces. We check clearances, venting routes and service access before picking the model, so it fits and stays easy to maintain.

  • Sound familiar?

    Describe the problem and we'll tell you what it usually is, what it costs to fix, and whether it's worth fixing.

Know your Oshawa house before you buy a furnace

The age of the house decides most of the hard parts of the job. Here is what we look for.

  • HousingWartime homes to north-end subdivisions

    Older brick and wartime houses near downtown, post-war bungalows, and newer homes in north-end areas like Windfields and Kedron.

  • VentingChimney or side wall

    Older furnaces vent up the chimney. High-efficiency models vent through PVC pipe out the side of the house, which changes what the chimney still does.

  • UtilitiesOshawa Power and Enbridge Gas

    We look at both bills before suggesting a heat pump, so the running cost comparison uses your real rates.

  • ClimateLake winds, cold winters

    Wind off Lake Ontario finds every gap in an old brick wall. The heat-loss number matters far more than the square footage.

Can your old ducts carry a heat pump? What we check first

A hybrid system pairs a cold-climate heat pump with a gas furnace. The heat pump handles cooling and most of the heating, and the furnace takes over in deep cold. It is often the best way to bring an older home's gas bill down without a full rebuild.

The catch is airflow. Heat pumps move more air per ton than the furnaces many older houses started with, and tired ducts can be the bottleneck. Skip the checks and you get noise, weak heat and a system that never reaches its rating. So before we quote, our licensed refrigeration techs look at:

  • Static pressure across the existing duct system
  • Supply and return sizes against the airflow the heat pump needs
  • Whether the new coil fits the plenum above the furnace
  • Panel space for a 240V circuit
  • An outdoor spot clear of roof drip lines and the neighbour's window

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a chimney liner when I replace my furnace in Oshawa?

Only if a gas water heater will keep using the chimney on its own. In that case a properly sized liner, or switching the water heater to side-wall venting, keeps the draft safe.

Can a high-efficiency furnace vent out the side of an older house?

Yes. Furnaces at 96% AFUE and above vent through PVC pipe out a side wall, and we plan the route to meet code clearances from windows, doors and the gas meter.

Will my old ducts work with a new heat pump?

Often they will, with some fixes. We test static pressure and duct sizes first, then size the heat pump to the airflow your ducts can deliver.

How much does furnace installation in Oshawa cost?

Expect a starting price of $3,300 installed for a high-efficiency furnace. Venting changes, a chimney liner or duct repairs add to that, and every item is listed on a guaranteed quote.

Is a hybrid heat pump worth it in an older Oshawa home?

For many older homes it is. The heat pump covers cooling and milder heating days, the furnace handles the deep cold, and gas use drops without replacing the ductwork.

Do you do furnace repair in Oshawa outside business hours?

Yes. Emergency calls are taken evenings, weekends and holidays, and our regular hours are Monday to Saturday 8 am to 8 pm and Sunday 10 am to 5 pm.
